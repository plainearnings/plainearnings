The $100 chart nets the $358M of other income (mostly the joint-venture gain) and $217M of interest expense inside the profit line rather than as a cost row: before that net $141M boost, operations alone kept just $0.18 of every $100. Humana reports two business segments — Insurance ($39.1B of revenue, a $303M operating loss) and CenterWell ($7.2B, $243M of operating income) — which overlap, so the revenue lines above are used as the chart's sources because they sum to total revenue. Net income attributable to Humana was $162M, after a $1M loss belonging to non-controlling interests.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

93.9%

Care costs ate the growth

Benefits expense rose 30% to $36.3B — faster than premiums — as new Medicare members ran higher benefit ratios and favorable prior-period reserve development shrank to $67M from $275M.

18.0M

Membership is booming

Total medical membership rose 20% to 18.0 million, with total Medicare membership up to 11.2 million — the growth behind the 26% revenue jump.

$9.50

Guidance still went up

Despite the adjusted loss, Humana raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to at least $9.50 (from $9.00) and pointed to near-record 2027 Star Ratings averaging 4.17 stars.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP profit includes a large non-cash gain from restructuring the CenterWell primary care joint ventures with a BlackRock affiliate (inside the $358M of other income) — excluding items like it, Humana lost $0.70 per share on an adjusted basis vs GAAP EPS of +$1.33.
  • The Insurance segment swung to a $303M operating loss (from a $251M profit), while CenterWell earned $243M.
