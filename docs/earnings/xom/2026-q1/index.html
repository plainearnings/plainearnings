Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$2.09

The underlying quarter was much stronger

Excluding identified items and an estimated $3.9B of unfavorable timing effects — derivatives marked to market before the physical shipments they hedge are delivered — earnings were $8,772M, or $2.09 a share, up from $7,584M a year ago. Those timing effects unwind in later quarters.

4.6M

Record Guyana output

Net production reached 4.6 million oil-equivalent barrels a day, with Guyana setting a quarterly record above 900 thousand gross barrels a day. Higher prices and margins, advantaged volume growth and $0.6B more of structural cost savings (now $15.6B cumulative since 2019) drove the underlying result.

$9.2B

Distributions outran cash flow

Operating cash flow was $8.7B ($13.8B excluding margin postings) and free cash flow $2.7B, yet ExxonMobil returned $9.2B to shareholders and declared a $1.03 second-quarter dividend. The period-end cash balance was $8.4B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Identified items cost $706M: losses on financial hedges that were settled while the physical shipments they covered were disrupted in the Middle East, so the offsets never landed in the same quarter. Excluding them, earnings were $4,889M ($1.16 a share).
  • Estimated timing effects cost a further $3,883M — mostly unsettled derivatives marked to quarter-end prices while the matching physical transactions complete in later periods. Excluding identified items and timing effects, earnings were $8,772M ($2.09 a share).
