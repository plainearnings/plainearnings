Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$104.52

Prices did the heavy lifting

Brent crude averaged $104.52 a barrel in the quarter (the U.S. marker, WTI, $93.21), and ExxonMobil's own U.S. crude realized $97.58. Sales rose 44% while production of 4,514 thousand oil-equivalent barrels a day was the highest in more than two decades, excluding Middle East disruptions.

$5.5B

Refining quadrupled its earnings

Energy Products earned $5,465M, up from $1,366M a year ago, as refining margins surged and diesel production hit a second-quarter record. Upstream added $7,927M, helped by record Permian output above 1.8 million barrels a day.

$1.03

Cash kept flowing back

Operating cash flow was $23.6B and free cash flow $17.2B. ExxonMobil returned $9.4B to shareholders ($4.3B of dividends, $5.1B of buybacks) and declared a third-quarter dividend of $1.03 a share.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP earnings include $1,079M of impairments among the company's 'identified items,' plus other identified charges including $1,365M added to financial reserves. Excluding identified items and timing effects, adjusted earnings were $14,680M, or $3.52 a share — only slightly above GAAP, because favorable timing effects offset much of the charges.
  • Results were delivered despite Middle East disruptions that took part of upstream production offline; a fifth Guyana floating production vessel is on plan to start up in Q4 2026, adding 250 thousand barrels a day of capacity.
