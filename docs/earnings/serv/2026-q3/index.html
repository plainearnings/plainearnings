Serve is an early-stage company: it spent about $21 for every $1 it took in this quarter, so the $100 view is scaled up accordingly — the loss was covered by cash raised earlier. Delivering the service cost $3.66 for every $1 of revenue, so the gross loss was $7.6M before any running costs.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+313%

Growth is real — and so is the scale gap

Revenue rose to $2.8M from $0.7M a year ago. The average fleet grew to 917 robots working daily (from 312), daily delivery volume among new partners tripled, and recurring revenue was 69% of the total. Management raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $12M.

-$7.6M

Each delivery still costs more than it earns

Cost of revenue was $10.4M against $2.8M of revenue — a gross loss of $7.6M, though the margin improved a lot from a year ago (when delivery cost $7.37 per $1 of revenue) and from Q2. The company says fleet margins improved more than 100 percentage points sequentially as volume spread the fixed costs.

-$59M

The bottom line is funded by the balance sheet

Total operating costs were $53.6M — research, admin, operations and sales — producing a $59.2M net loss. A large share is stock pay: on the company's non-GAAP basis (leaving out stock compensation and similar items) the loss was $44.6M. Serve held $198M in cash and securities at quarter end.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP costs include substantial non-cash stock-based compensation: the company's non-GAAP figures put Q3 operating costs at $39.2M vs $53.6M GAAP, and the non-GAAP net loss at $44.6M vs $59.2M GAAP.
