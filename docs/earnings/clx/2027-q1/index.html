Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+33%

A rebound quarter, flattered by last year

About 18 points of the growth was organic — mostly retailers rebuilding stock after drawing down inventories during last year's ERP system transition, a one-off drag Clorox is now lapping. GOJO added roughly 15 points more.

$918M

GOJO changes the shape of Clorox

Health and Wellness is now 48% of all sales after the GOJO deal brought Purell's professional hygiene business in-house. Integrating it — and paying down the debt taken on — is the story of the next few quarters.

40.4%

Costs are still biting

Gross margin fell 130 basis points to 40.4% on higher commodity, manufacturing and logistics costs — including about 50 basis points from writing up GOJO's inventory to fair value. Cost savings and volume only partly offset it.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GOJO transaction costs — mainly integration spending and the inventory step-up (writing acquired stock up to fair value, which then flows through cost of sales) — weighed on GAAP results; excluding them and similar items, adjusted EPS was $1.55 vs the $1.39 GAAP figure.
  • The year-ago quarter was depressed by retailers drawing down inventory built up ahead of Clorox's ERP system transition — Clorox estimates that comparison added about $0.90 to this quarter's EPS growth rate.
