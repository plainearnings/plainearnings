Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+5.4%

Growth in every business

Revenue rose 5.4% to $108.4B. Health Services — the pharmacy-benefit business that negotiates drug prices for employers and health plans — grew fastest, up 9.5% to $54.0B on drug mix and brand inflation, while store revenue dipped 2.0% on drug-price cuts and reimbursement pressure, partly offset by the Rite Aid stores and prescription files CVS bought in 2025.

91.3%

Aetna's recovery carried profit

The medical benefit ratio — the share of premiums paid out as medical care — improved to 91.3% from 92.8% a year ago. Health Care Benefits adjusted operating income nearly tripled to $912M from $314M, and company-wide adjusted operating income rose 21% to $4.2B.

$1.68

Back in profit — and guidance raised

GAAP EPS was $1.68, vs a $3.13 loss a year ago that included a $5.7B goodwill impairment (writing down the value of a past acquisition). Adjusted EPS was $2.09 vs $1.60. CVS raised full-year 2026 guidance — adjusted EPS to at least $8.20 and operating cash flow to at least $15.5B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating expenses include a $193M charge for an estimated guarantee liability tied to Omnicare, the long-term-care pharmacy business CVS separated from in September 2025. CVS excludes it from adjusted earnings.
  • The gap between GAAP operating income ($3,566M) and adjusted operating income ($4,194M) also reflects $425M of amortization of intangible assets from past acquisitions and $13M of acquisition-integration costs, partly offset by a $3M net realized capital gain.
