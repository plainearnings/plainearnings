Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$1.16

The underlying quarter was solid

Adjusted EPS was $1.16, up 12% from a year ago, and adjusted revenue was stable at about $5.3B — the GAAP drop is the securities repositioning, not the business shrinking.

-$1.4B

A deliberate, one-off hit

Truist sold securities at a $1,447M loss to reposition its balance sheet after selling the RAC auto-loan book, and agreed on October 14 to sell $7.7B of LightStream unsecured loans as well.

1.25%

Credit cover got thinner — by design

The allowance for loan and lease losses fell to 1.25% of loans from 1.54% a year ago, mostly because the riskier RAC loans left the book. Charge-offs were 0.77% annualized, including RAC effects.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $1,447M pre-tax loss on the sale of available-for-sale securities, booked as part of a balance-sheet repositioning after the Regional Acceptance Corporation (RAC) loan sale — the main reason GAAP EPS was $0.56 vs adjusted EPS of $1.16.
  • A $581M pre-tax benefit inside credit costs from releasing loan-loss reserves after the RAC loan sale, plus $106M of other RAC sale impacts and $40M of RAC sale costs in expenses. Excluding all flagged items, net income available to common shareholders was $1.42B instead of $684M.

Educational only — not financial advice. For a bank, the provision for credit losses plays the role of cost of revenue. This quarter it was a $253M benefit (money added back, not spent) because selling the RAC loan book released $581M of reserves — so the amount after credit costs ($4,000M) is larger than revenue itself. Net income of $799M is before $115M of preferred dividends, leaving $684M for common shareholders.
