Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+12%

Both express segments grew

U.S. Domestic revenue rose 10% to $14,710M and International rose 17% to $7,465M on higher package yields, volumes and freight weight — even with global trade-policy headwinds.

$1.21 vs $3.74

GAAP and adjusted tell two stories

GAAP EPS was $1.21 after the $1,238M paper loss on the Freight stake and a $627M debt-extinguishment gain. Stripping those and restructuring out, adjusted EPS was $3.74 on $896M of adjusted net income.

$2.0B

Buybacks stepped up

FedEx repurchased $2.0B of stock in June–September and raised its calendar 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $18.00–$18.60.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • FedEx completed the spin-off of FedEx Freight on June 1, 2026 and changed its fiscal year-end from May 31 to December 31 — so this is calendar Q3 2026, and Freight is no longer in continuing operations. FedEx kept a 19.9% Freight stake, marked to market each quarter: a $1.2B loss this quarter.
  • Operating expenses include $130M of restructuring, exit and other costs; the tax line includes a $263M deferred tax benefit tied to the FedEx Supply Chain business (sold October 1, 2026), which is why the GAAP tax charge is only $134M.
  • A $627M pre-tax gain on extinguishing debt early sits inside other non-operating income — FedEx used the ~$4.1B dividend received from Freight around the spin-off to tender for ~$4.9B of its bonds.
