An unusual quarter: this $100 is $100 of everything that came in — about $25 of rent, $3 of other property income and $72 from selling the California portfolio. Camden's property revenue on its own was $381M.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$974M

A big property sale dominates the quarter

On July 29 Camden sold its whole California portfolio — 11 communities, 3,620 homes — for about $1.6B and booked a $974M gain. About $0.9B of the money repaid borrowing. The gain is why EPS jumped to $8.71 from $1.00 a year ago.

95.6%

The apartments themselves were steady

For homes Camden owned in both periods, revenue was almost flat (-0.1% YoY) and occupancy was 95.6%. Renewing tenants paid 3.6% more, while new leases signed for 2.0% less. Total revenue fell mainly because the sold California homes are gone, not because the remaining homes weakened.

$1.70

The REIT measure barely moved

Funds From Operations was $1.70 a share, up from $1.67 a year ago, and Core FFO was $1.71, almost flat. FFO strips out the sale gain and the $86M land write-down and adds back depreciation, so it shows a steadier business than the $900M headline profit suggests. Camden also bought three communities for $305M in the quarter.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Gain on sale of properties: $974M from selling the California portfolio (11 communities, 3,620 homes) for about $1.6B on July 29, 2026. Without this gain, Camden would have reported a loss this quarter — it is a one-off, not rent.
  • Impairment: Camden wrote down land development assets by $86M (recording that the land is worth less than its book value). Camden treats this as non-core and excludes it from Core FFO.
