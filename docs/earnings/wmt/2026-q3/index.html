Walmart — Q3 FY26 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q3 FY26 · Quarter ended 2025-10-31
Reported 2025-11-20

Walmart's revenue rose 5.8% to $179.5B and its U.S. stores earned 6.3% more — but a $0.7B one-time stock charge at PhonePe held reported operating profit flat, while a $2.1B investment gain lifted headline profit 34% to $6.1B.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+27%

Online sales keep compounding

Global eCommerce grew 27%, with every segment up more than 20%, led by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery and the marketplace. U.S. comparable sales rose 4.5% — and Walmart raised its full-year sales and profit outlook on the strength of the quarter.

$0.7B

A one-time charge at PhonePe

International operating income fell 41.7% to $702M because of a roughly $0.7B non-cash stock-compensation charge at PhonePe, Walmart's Indian payments business, ahead of a potential IPO. Adjusted for it and currency, group operating income rose 8.0%.

+34%

Paper gains lifted the headline

A $2,081M gain on equity and other investments turned a flat operating quarter into a 34% rise in headline profit, to $6.1B. Adjusted EPS of $0.62 — which strips the gain out — is the cleaner read on the stores.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other (gains) and losses was a $2,081M gain — mostly fair-value moves on equity investments (a year ago it was a $132M loss). Adjusted EPS of $0.62 excludes a $0.20-per-share net-of-tax gain from these items.
  • A roughly $0.7B non-cash share-based compensation charge at PhonePe (India) sits inside operating expenses, ahead of a potential PhonePe IPO — it is why reported operating income fell 0.2% while the adjusted figure rose 8.0% in constant currency.
