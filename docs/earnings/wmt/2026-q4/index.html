Walmart keeps just $2.22 of every $100 — but on $190.7B of quarterly revenue that is still $4.2B. This quarter $1.44 of every $100 went to paper losses on investment stakes; the stores themselves earned 10.8% more than a year ago.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+10.8%

Stores earned faster than sales

Operating income rose 10.8% to $8.7B — twice the pace of sales — as gross margin improved and eCommerce economics got better, led by Walmart U.S. and International.

−$2.1B

Paper losses cut the headline

A $2,117M loss on equity and other investments turned a strong store quarter into a 19% decline in headline profit. Adjusted EPS, which strips the loss out, was $0.74 versus GAAP EPS of $0.53.

$30B

New buyback, bigger dividend

Walmart announced a new $30B share-repurchase authorization in February 2026 and raised its annual dividend to $0.99 per share.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other (gains) and losses was a $2,117M loss — mostly fair-value moves on equity investments (a year ago it was a $294M loss). Adjusted EPS of $0.74 excludes a $0.21 net-of-tax loss from these items.
  • Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale shifted on the calendar — nine days fell in Q3 this year versus five last year — which moved some International sales out of the fourth quarter.
