Walmart keeps just $2.70 of every $100 — but on $169.6B of quarterly revenue that is still $4.6B. A year ago the same quarter kept only about $0.30 of every $100, after a $4.75B paper loss on investment stakes.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.2%

Stores earned faster than sales

Operating income rose 8.2% to $6.7B — faster than sales — as the gross margin rate improved 21 basis points, membership income grew, and eCommerce losses shrank.

+27%

eCommerce and ads surged

Global eCommerce sales grew 27%, led by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery and the marketplace, and the global advertising business grew 28% — newer, higher-margin income streams growing far faster than the stores.

Raised

Full-year guidance lifted

Walmart raised its FY25 outlook: net sales growth of 4.8% to 5.1% and adjusted operating income growth of 8.5% to 9.25% in constant currency.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other (gains) and losses was a $132M loss on equity and other investments — mostly fair-value moves. Adjusted EPS of $0.58 excludes a $0.01 net-of-tax loss from these items. A year ago the same line was a $4,750M loss, which cut that quarter's attributable profit to $453M.
