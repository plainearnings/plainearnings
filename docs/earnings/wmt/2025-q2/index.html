Walmart — Q2 FY25 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.5%

Operating income outgrew sales

Operating income rose 8.5% to $7,940M on 4.8% revenue growth — the gross margin rate widened 43 basis points, membership income grew 16%, and global eCommerce sales rose 21%, led by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery.

$1.2B

A paper loss, not a store problem

Below the operating line, Walmart booked a $1,162M loss on equity and other investments — mostly fair-value moves on stakes it holds. A year ago the same line was a $3,905M gain; that swing is the whole reason headline profit fell 43% while the stores improved.

$0.67

Guidance went up anyway

GAAP EPS was $0.56, but adjusted EPS was $0.67 excluding the $0.11-per-share net loss on investments. With this release Walmart raised its FY25 outlook: net sales up 3.75% to 4.75% and adjusted operating income up 6.5% to 8.0% in constant currency.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other (gains) and losses was a $1,162M loss this quarter versus a $3,905M gain a year ago — unrealized fair-value moves on equity investments Walmart holds. Adjusted EPS of $0.67 excludes the net-of-tax effect ($0.11 per share) of those investment moves.
