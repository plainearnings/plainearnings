Walmart keeps just $2.91 of every $100 — but on $180.6B of quarterly revenue that is still $5.3B. The stores themselves earned 8.3% more than a year ago; a $294M paper loss on investment stakes held the headline profit down.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.3%

Stores earned faster than sales

Operating income rose 8.3% to $7.9B — twice the pace of sales — as the gross margin rate improved 53 basis points, led by Walmart U.S., membership income grew, and eCommerce economics got better.

−$294M

Paper losses cut the headline

A $294M loss on equity and other investments, versus an $813M gain a year ago, turned a strong store quarter into a 4.4% decline in headline profit. Adjusted EPS, which strips the loss out, was $0.66 versus GAAP EPS of $0.65.

$0.94

Dividend raised 13%

Walmart raised its annual dividend 13% to $0.94 per share — its largest increase in over a decade — after generating $36.4B in operating cash flow for the full year.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other (gains) and losses was a $294M loss — mostly fair-value moves on equity investments (a year ago it was an $813M gain). Adjusted EPS of $0.66 excludes a $0.02 net-of-tax loss from these items.
  • A $99M gain from opioid-related legal matters — proceeds from settlement of a shareholder derivative lawsuit, recorded in Corporate and support — boosted reported operating income. Adjusted operating income of $7,760M excludes it.
  • Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale shifted on the calendar, which moved some International sales into Q3 and held back Q4 — International net sales fell 0.7% in dollars but rose 5.7% in constant currency.
