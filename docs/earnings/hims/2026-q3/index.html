The striking thing: the company spends $27.77 of every $100 on marketing and another $17.01 on G&A — together more than the entire gross profit left after product costs. A year ago, cost of revenue took only ~$26 of every $100; now it takes ~$43, and that 17-point margin collapse is what turned a profitable quarter into a loss.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+49%

The growth is real, but it changed shape

Revenue +49% with international sales up 321% — but that growth came with acquisitions, a much higher cost base, and a gross margin down 17 points. This is no longer the high-margin U.S. subscription story of a year ago.

2.79M

The loss is a spending story, not a demand story

Subscribers grew 13% to 2.79M and each subscriber now pays $105/month (up 30%). Demand is healthy — the problem is that opex grew $145M while gross profit grew only $67M, with G&A nearly doubling.

$3.13–3.15B

Guidance got more cautious on profit, not on sales

Full-year revenue was tightened to $3.13–3.15B while Adjusted EBITDA guidance was reduced to $257–277M. Management is spending ahead of Q4 platform expansions it says will set up 2027 — investors have to take on trust that the spending pays off.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring charges ($3.8M in Q3; $41.9M in the first nine months): costs from the February 2026 strategic shift in the U.S. weight-loss offering — moving to branded GLP-1 drugs and offering compounded GLP-1s only on a limited scale. The company adds these back in Adjusted EBITDA.
  • Acquisition, transaction and investment earn-out costs ($35.4M in Q3; $77.6M in nine months): deal costs and earn-outs tied to the international acquisitions behind the rest-of-world surge. Also added back in Adjusted EBITDA.
  • Legal contingencies ($62.5M in the first nine months, $0 in Q3): booked earlier in 2026, not in this quarter's numbers — but it is why the nine-month net loss (−$211.4M) is far worse than Q3 alone.
  • Stock-based compensation ($64.5M in Q3; $143.5M in nine months): a real, recurring cost of paying employees in shares — excluded from Adjusted EBITDA, but it dilutes shareholders.
  • Adjusted vs GAAP gap: Adjusted EBITDA of $82.4M vs a GAAP net loss of −$33.0M. The bridge is mostly stock comp ($64.5M), acquisition costs ($35.4M), depreciation ($38.2M) and the tax benefit. Adjusted net income is only $6.5M — even on management's preferred basis, the quarter was barely profitable.
