Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+38%

Growth sped up again — mostly abroad

Revenue rose 38% to $753M. US sales grew 16% to $622M, while sales outside the US jumped from $8M to $131M after the Eucalyptus deal closed in June. Subscribers reached 2.89M, up 19%, and each subscriber brought in $92 a month on average, up 21%. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to $3.1B–$3.3B.

64%

Each sale got less profitable

Gross margin fell from 76% to 64%. The cost of revenue more than doubled, up 112% to $272M, because weight-loss offerings — including GLP-1 drugs (medicines first used for diabetes, now widely used for weight loss) — cost more to buy, package and ship, and ship more often, and the fast-growing international mix costs more to serve.

-$86M

One-off costs tipped it into a loss

The bottom line swung from a $43M profit a year ago to an $86M loss. General and administrative costs alone rose $98M, including a $48M legal charge and $29M of deal costs. Leaving out those and similar items, the company's own Adjusted EBITDA measure was still positive at $60M, down from $82M a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • General and administrative costs include a $48M legal contingencies charge in Q2 alone ($63M in the first half) that the company treats as non-recurring in its Adjusted EBITDA — it is the main reason that line jumped 146% YoY.
  • General and administrative costs also include $29M of acquisition and transaction-related costs in Q2, largely tied to buying Eucalyptus — an Australian digital-health platform bought for a $969M accounting purchase price ($225M cash up front, the rest deferred and contingent) that closed in June 2026.
  • Operations and support includes $5M of restructuring and other charges in Q2 ($38M in the first half, including inventory write-downs in cost of revenue in Q1) tied to the company's 2026 shift in its US weight-loss offering toward branded GLP-1 medicines, with compounded GLP-1s offered only on a limited scale.
