Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+31%

Growth, much of it bought

Revenue grew 31% to $3.0B — but $388M of it came from the newly acquired CyberArk and Chronosphere, so organic growth was slower than the headline.

+60%

The platform bet is working

Next-Generation Security ARR grew 60% to $8.1B (including $1.6B from the acquisitions), and remaining performance obligations rose 36% to $18.4B.

-$177M

A paper loss, not a cash loss

GAAP showed a $177M loss, driven by $517M of stock pay and $478M of acquisition costs and amortization. Operating cash flow was $871M in the same quarter — the loss consumed no cash.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based compensation was $517M, acquisition-related costs $198M and amortization of acquired intangible assets $280M — together they are the entire gap between the $177M GAAP net loss and $684M of non-GAAP net income.
  • Acquisitions did heavy lifting: CyberArk and Chronosphere contributed $388M of the quarter's revenue, $1.6B of Next-Generation Security ARR and $1.8B of remaining performance obligations.
  • Excluding one-offs and stock pay, Palo Alto earned $684M (non-GAAP) — the underlying business was profitable even as GAAP showed a loss.
