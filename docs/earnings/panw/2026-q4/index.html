Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+63%

The platform bet is working

Next-Generation Security ARR — subscriptions to its newer software platforms — grew 63% to $9.10B, adding nearly $1B in this single quarter.

−$282M

A paper loss, not a cash loss

GAAP shows a $282M loss, driven by deal-related charges below the operating line. Operating cash flow was $1.4B in the same quarter — the loss consumed no cash.

$21.2B

Future revenue is locked in

Remaining performance obligations — contracted revenue not yet delivered — grew 34% to $21.2B, more than 6× this quarter's revenue.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $441M other expense was mostly non-cash: fair-value changes on convertible notes acquired with CyberArk and the hedges against them. It is the entire reason a profitable operating quarter became a net loss.
  • G&A jumped to $226M from $27M a year ago on CyberArk acquisition and integration costs, including share-based pay tied to the deal.
  • Excluding one-offs and stock pay, Palo Alto earned $853M (non-GAAP) — the business itself was strongly profitable.
