Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+26%

Ads kept growing fast

Advertising revenue rose 26% to $50.1B: ad impressions grew 14% and the average price per ad rose 10%. The Family of Apps earned $25.0B of operating profit.

$15.9B

A one-time tax charge crushed reported profit

A non-cash tax charge from the new U.S. tax law (remeasuring deferred tax assets under the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax) pushed Meta's tax rate to 87%. Reported net income was $2.7B; excluding the charge it was $18.6B, and EPS was $7.25 rather than $1.05.

$19.4B

The AI bill keeps climbing

Capital spending was $19.4B in the quarter, including finance leases, and free cash flow was $10.6B. For 2025 Meta raised its capex plan to $70-72B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A one-time, non-cash income tax charge of $15.93B — from remeasuring deferred tax assets after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act changed how the U.S. Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax applies — pushed Meta's effective tax rate to 87% and cut net income to $2.71B (EPS $1.05). Excluding the charge, net income was $18.64B (EPS $7.25) and the tax rate was 14%. Meta expects the law to significantly reduce its U.S. federal cash tax payments for the remainder of 2025 and in future years.
