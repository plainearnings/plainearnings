Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-12%

The home market is shrinking fast

Americas comparable sales fell 12% and regional revenue fell 8% to $1,617M. China grew just 4% as reported (−2% in constant currency). Total comparable sales fell 9% — this is a demand problem, not a supply one.

$134.5M

A tariff refund dressed up the margin

Gross margin rose 200 basis points to 60.5% only because tariff refunds added roughly 560 points. Underneath, product margins fell on higher tariffs and heavier markdowns — the refund made a weak quarter look respectable.

-5–7%

The full-year outlook was cut

Management cut the full-year outlook to revenue of $10.35–10.50B and EPS of $9.48–9.73, and guided the next quarter down 10–11%. A new CEO, Heidi O'Neill, started the week after this report — she inherits a turnaround.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Lululemon received $134.5M of IEEPA tariff refunds in the quarter, booked as a reduction of cost of goods sold — worth about 560 basis points of gross margin and, with $4.1M of related interest, $0.86 of EPS. Without the refunds, gross margin would have fallen.
  • Proxy-contest costs of $13.4M sit inside SG&A ($24.8M across the first two quarters).
  • Guidance was cut with this release: fiscal 2026 revenue is now expected at $10.350–10.500B (down 5–7%) and EPS at $9.48–$9.73, assuming no further tariff refunds.
