Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

~$3B

Vantage is being sold

DTE agreed to sell its non-utility Vantage business for about $3B, sharpening its focus on the regulated utilities, where it plans about $44B of investment over the next five years — including $6.1B in 2026 alone.

$468M

The electric utility does the heavy lifting

The Electric segment earned $468M of the $470M total. Gas lost $30M in its seasonally quiet quarter, Vantage added $110M and Energy Trading $23M, while Corporate and Other lost $101M.

$107M

A tax benefit flattered the bottom line

DTE booked a $107M income tax benefit rather than a tax bill this quarter, which is why $363M of pre-tax profit became $470M of net income. Operating EPS, excluding a small mark-to-market item, was $2.30 vs the reported $2.25.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Reported EPS of $2.25 includes a $15M pre-tax mark-to-market loss in Energy Trading (derivatives revalued without revaluing the contracts they hedge). Excluding it, operating EPS was $2.30.
  • DTE agreed to sell DTE Vantage for approximately $3B to investors advised by J.P. Morgan Asset Management — the sale is expected to close in the first half of 2027 and is not in this quarter's profit.
