Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$750M

Hurricanes hit underwriting

After-tax underwriting earnings fell to $750M from $2,422M a year ago. Hurricane Helene alone cost about $715M in the reinsurance business and about $260M at GEICO before tax — while GEICO's premiums earned still grew about 5% on higher prices per policy.

$26.3B

Paper gains swung the headline

Headline profit swung from a $12.8B loss a year ago to $26.3B — almost entirely the stock portfolio. A year ago investment losses were $29.8B before tax; this quarter they were $20.5B of gains, including about $18.4B after tax realized on sales, mostly trimming Apple.

$320B

The cash pile set another record

Cash and Treasury Bills reached about $320B at September 30 — $32.3B of cash plus $288.0B of Treasury Bills — as Berkshire sold far more stock than it bought. Insurance float was about $174B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment gains of $20,514M before tax ($16,161M after tax) are mostly paper gains. Of the after-tax total, about $18.4B was realized on sales of investments and $2.2B was unrealized losses on stocks Berkshire still owns. Next quarter the unrealized part can just as easily reverse — a year ago this line was a $29,778M pre-tax loss.
  • Hurricane Helene struck in late September: roughly $715M of pre-tax losses in the reinsurance group and about $260M at GEICO. After-tax insurance underwriting earnings fell to $750M from $2,422M a year ago.
  • Currency moves on foreign debt: revaluing Berkshire's euro-, pound- and yen-denominated borrowings produced a loss of about $1.1B after tax inside the 'Other' operating earnings line, versus a gain of about $447M a year ago.
