Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$13.5B

Operating earnings rose 34%

The businesses themselves earned $13.5B after tax, up from $10.1B — insurance underwriting more than tripled to $2.4B, and manufacturing, service and retailing earned $3.6B.

+$17.3B

Paper gains did the heavy lifting

Investment gains of $17.3B after tax — $9.2B of it unrealized gains on stocks Berkshire still holds, plus $8.2B realized on sales — turned a strong operating quarter into $30.8B of headline profit.

$176B

The float keeps growing

Insurance float — the premiums Berkshire holds and invests before it has to pay claims — reached about $176B, up $5B since the end of 2024.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment gains are mostly paper gains: $9.2B of the quarter's $17.3B after-tax investment gains were unrealized — stocks Berkshire still owns went up in value. Next quarter they can just as easily go down.
  • 'Other' operating earnings included about $331M of foreign-currency gains on non-dollar debt, versus a $1.1B loss a year ago — one reason that line swung from a $678M loss to a $1.4B profit.
