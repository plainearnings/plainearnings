Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$10.2B

Operating earnings fell 30%

The businesses themselves earned $10.2B after tax, down from $14.5B — insurance underwriting dropped to $1.6B from $3.4B, and insurance investment income fell to $3.1B as interest rates came down.

+$13.5B

Paper gains did the heavy lifting

Investment gains of $13.5B after tax — $9.6B of it unrealized gains on stocks Berkshire still holds, plus $3.9B realized on sales — turned a soft operating quarter into $19.2B of headline profit.

−$4.5B

Two big stakes written down

Berkshire recorded $4.5B of after-tax write-downs on its Kraft Heinz and Occidental Petroleum stakes — an admission both are worth less than it paid — inside equity-method losses of $5.3B before tax.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other-than-temporary impairments: Berkshire wrote down its equity-method stakes in Kraft Heinz and Occidental Petroleum by $4.5B after tax in the quarter ($8.3B for the full year). The charge sits inside equity-method earnings, not operating earnings.
  • Investment gains are mostly paper gains: $9.6B of the quarter's $13.5B after-tax investment gains were unrealized — stocks Berkshire still owns went up in value. Next quarter they can just as easily go down.
  • 'Other' operating earnings included about $617M of foreign-currency gains on non-dollar debt, versus $1.2B a year ago — one reason that line fell from $1.8B to $159M.
