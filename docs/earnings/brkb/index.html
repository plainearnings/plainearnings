The striking thing: the stock portfolio added $11.31 of every $100 — nearly as much as the $13.46 the operating businesses kept. That is why Berkshire's headline profit swings with the market, and why Buffett tells investors to watch operating earnings instead. Rows may not add exactly due to rounding.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

−43%

GEICO is the weak spot

Insurance underwriting profit fell 43% to $1,352M, and every one of Berkshire's three insurance groups earned less. GEICO's loss ratio jumped to 76.6% as claims costs rose — and there were no big catastrophes to blame. Insurance investment income also fell 14% as lower rates cut what the float earns.

$9.6B

The buyback is the headline

Berkshire repurchased $9.6B of its own stock in the quarter — more than the $4.8B it bought in the entire first half, bringing the nine-month total to $14.4B. With the cash pile still at $377.4B, management is choosing its own shares over new deals and T-bills.

−28%

The 28% profit drop is mostly the market, not the businesses

Net earnings fell to $22.1B from $30.8B, but $7.9B of that gap is smaller after-tax investment gains ($9.4B vs $17.3B). Operating earnings — the number Buffett says matters — fell only 5.8%, and BNSF (+19%), energy (+8%) and manufacturing/service/retailing (+12%) all grew.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • No Kraft Heinz impairment this quarter. The $3.76B after-tax impairment on Berkshire's Kraft Heinz stake was booked in Q2 2025, so it flatters the nine-month comparison but does not touch Q3.
  • "Other" earnings include a currency swing: a loss on Berkshire's non-U.S.-dollar debt this quarter versus a gain a year ago — one reason the "Other" line fell from $1,381M to $1,236M.
  • No big catastrophe losses this year. Berkshire had no significant catastrophe events in the first nine months of 2026; the same period in 2025 carried about $850M of after-tax catastrophe losses. In other words, the underwriting decline happened despite a calmer disaster year — it is a claims-cost story (GEICO), not a hurricane story.
  • A $16.2B acquisition spree in nine months. Berkshire spent $16,205M acquiring businesses in the first nine months of 2026, versus $918M in the same period last year — the 10-Q does not break out the deals by name in the cash-flow statement, but the pace is a sharp change from the quiet 2025.
