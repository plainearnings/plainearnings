Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.2B

Operating earnings rose 39%

The businesses themselves earned $11.2B after tax, up from $8.1B a year ago — insurance underwriting nearly tripled to $2.6B as GEICO's loss ratio fell 10.5 points to 72.5%, and insurance investment income rose to $2.6B as Treasury Bill yields lifted interest income.

$12.7B

Headline profit fell — the year-ago bar was the stock market

Headline profit was $12.7B, down from $35.5B a year ago even though the businesses improved — investment gains were $1.9B before tax, versus $34.8B a year ago. Berkshire sold about $20B of stocks in the quarter, mostly Apple: Apple was worth $135.4B at March 31, down from $174.3B at year-end.

$189B

The cash pile set a record

Cash, equivalents and Treasury Bills reached about $189B at March 31 — Berkshire received $20.0B from stock sales in the quarter and spent only $2.7B buying, parking the money in Treasury Bills. Insurance float was about $168B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment gains of $1,876M before tax ($1,480M after tax) were mostly locked in, not paper: after-tax realized gains on sales were $11.2B in the quarter — taxable gains were $14.2B before tax on about $20.0B of stock sales, mainly trimming Apple — partly offset by a $9.7B after-tax fall in unrealized gains, much of it those same Apple gains leaving the unsold pile. The unrealized part can just as easily reverse next quarter.
  • No significant catastrophe events hit in the first quarter of 2024, while a year ago a cyclone and floods in New Zealand cost about $350M after tax. GEICO's pre-tax underwriting earnings jumped to $1,928M from $703M as its loss ratio fell to 72.5% from 83.0%.
  • Currency moves on foreign debt: revaluing Berkshire's non-dollar borrowings produced a gain of about $597M after tax inside the 'Other' operating earnings line, versus a $17M loss a year ago.
  • Berkshire bought the remaining 20% of Pilot Travel Centers in January 2024 for $2.6B, taking its ownership to 100% — Pilot, the truck-stop chain, contributed $12.5B of revenue in the quarter on the segment basis.
