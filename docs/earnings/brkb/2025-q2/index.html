Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.2B

Operating earnings eased 4%

The businesses themselves earned $11.2B after tax, down from $11.6B — insurance underwriting slipped to $2.0B, while manufacturing, service and retailing rose to $3.6B.

$12.4B

Headline profit is the noisy number

Net earnings fell from $30.3B to $12.4B mostly because investment gains shrank from $23.9B to $6.4B pre-tax — market moves, not business performance.

$344B

The cash pile keeps climbing

Cash, equivalents and Treasury Bills reached about $344B at June 30 — Berkshire is still waiting for a big enough deal at the right price. Insurance float was about $174B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Berkshire wrote down its Kraft Heinz stake: a $3.76B after-tax impairment is the biggest piece of the quarter's $4.7B of pre-tax equity-method losses.
  • Investment gains included $1.5B of paper gains on stocks Berkshire still owns and $4.2B realized on sales (after tax) — far below the $23.9B of pre-tax gains a year ago, which is most of why headline profit fell so sharply.
  • 'Other' operating earnings included $877M of foreign-currency losses on non-dollar debt, versus a $446M gain a year ago.
