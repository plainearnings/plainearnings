Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$9.6B

Operating earnings fell 14%

The businesses themselves earned $9.6B after tax, down from $11.2B − insurance underwriting dropped to $1.3B from $2.6B after roughly $860M of after-tax losses from the Southern California wildfires, and a currency swing on foreign debt dragged 'Other' earnings from $1.1B to $41M.

−$6.4B

A paper loss on the stock pile

Investment losses of $6.4B before tax were mostly unrealized: stocks Berkshire still holds fell $6.8B in value, partly offset by small gains on stocks sold during the quarter ($4.7B of sales). After tax, the investment loss was $5.0B.

$342.4B

The cash pile hit another record

Cash and Treasury Bills at the insurance and other businesses rose to $342.4B, up from $330.8B at year-end — Berkshire sold $4.7B of stocks against $3.2B of purchases, parked the money in Treasury Bills, and bought back none of its own shares.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Southern California wildfires: the insurance businesses took about $1.1B of pre-tax catastrophe losses ($860M after tax) — the quarter's only significant catastrophe event, versus none a year ago. GEICO itself improved (pre-tax underwriting profit of $2,173M, up from $1,928M); the wildfire losses pushed the primary and reinsurance groups into underwriting losses.
  • Currency swing on foreign debt: revaluing Berkshire's euro-, pound- and yen-denominated borrowings produced a $936M pre-tax loss this quarter versus a $781M gain a year ago. The pre-tax loss sits inside selling, general and administrative expenses; after tax ($713M loss versus a $597M gain) it is the main reason 'Other' operating earnings fell from $1,078M to $41M.
  • Investment losses are mostly paper losses. Of the $5,038M after-tax investment losses, about $7.4B was a fall in unrealized gains on stocks Berkshire still holds, partly offset by $2.4B of after-tax realized gains on stocks sold. Next quarter the unrealized part can just as easily reverse.
