Investment gains added $25.47 of every $100 of revenue — a net of $47.0B in after-tax gains locked in by selling stock, mostly Apple, minus a $28.2B fall in paper gains on stocks still held. Without them, the businesses and everything else kept $6.93. Rows may not add exactly due to rounding.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.6B

Operating earnings rose 15%

The businesses themselves earned $11.6B after tax, up from $10.0B a year ago — insurance underwriting nearly doubled to $2.3B as GEICO's loss ratio fell about 10 points, and insurance investment income rose to $3.3B on Treasury Bill yields.

$30.3B

Selling Apple swelled the headline

Headline profit was $30.3B, down from $35.9B a year ago even though the businesses improved — investment gains were $23.9B before tax, less than last year's $33.1B. Berkshire sold roughly half its Apple stake: Apple was worth $84.2B at June 30, down from $174.3B at year-end.

$277B

The cash pile set a record

Cash, equivalents and Treasury Bills reached about $277B at June 30 — Berkshire received $97.1B from stock sales in the first six months and spent only $4.3B buying. Insurance float was about $169B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment gains of $23,857M before tax ($18,750M after tax) were mostly locked in, not paper: after-tax realized gains on sales were $47.0B in the quarter — taxable gains were $59.6B before tax, mainly from trimming Apple — partly offset by a $28.2B after-tax fall in unrealized gains, much of it those same Apple gains leaving the unsold pile. The unrealized part can just as easily reverse next quarter.
  • No significant catastrophe events hit in the first six months of 2024, while a year ago they cost about $450M after tax in the first half. GEICO's pre-tax underwriting earnings jumped to $1,786M from $514M as its loss ratio fell to 74.1% from 84.3%.
  • Currency moves on foreign debt: revaluing Berkshire's non-dollar borrowings produced a gain of about $446M after tax inside the 'Other' operating earnings line, similar to a $465M gain a year ago.
