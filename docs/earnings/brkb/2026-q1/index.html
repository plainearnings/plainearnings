Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.3B

Operating earnings up 18%

The businesses themselves earned $11.3B after tax, up from $9.6B — insurance underwriting jumped to $1.7B with no significant catastrophe losses, versus $860M of storm losses a year ago.

−$1.6B

A paper loss on the stock pile

Investment losses of $1.6B were mostly unrealized: the value of stocks Berkshire still holds fell $3.3B, partly offset by $1.7B of gains on stocks sold during the quarter ($24.1B of sales).

$9.5B

OxyChem joins the family

Berkshire closed its roughly $9.5B purchase of Occidental's chemical business on January 2, 2026 — and the cash pile at its insurance and other businesses still stood at $373.5B at quarter-end.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment losses are mostly paper losses. Of the $1,605M pre-tax investment losses, $3,291M was a fall in unrealized gains on stocks Berkshire still holds, partly offset by $1,652M of gains on stocks sold. Next quarter the unrealized part can just as easily reverse.
  • No repeat of last year's catastrophe hit: the first quarter of 2025 carried about $860M of after-tax losses from significant catastrophe events; this quarter had none.
