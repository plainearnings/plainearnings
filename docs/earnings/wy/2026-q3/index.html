Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$1.39B

Wood Products carried sales

Wood Products sales rose 13% to $1,385M and its contribution to earnings swung to +$32M from −$19M a year ago, as lumber and panel markets improved.

$248M

Cash earnings rose

Adjusted EBITDA — a non-GAAP cash-earnings yardstick — rose 14% to $248M even as GAAP earnings fell, because last year's GAAP result held one-off gains that did not repeat.

$146M

Selling non-core land

Weyerhaeuser is selling about 81,000 acres in Arkansas for $146M (closing expected in Q4), while monetizing roughly 340,000 forest carbon credits so far this year and starting construction of its first biocarbon facility.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • No special items in Q3 2026: GAAP net earnings of $61M equal net earnings before special items. A year ago, other operating income included a $29M gain on a lumber mill sale and $34M of insurance recoveries, which flatter the year-ago comparison.
  • After quarter-end, Weyerhaeuser agreed to sell about 81,000 acres of non-core Arkansas timberlands for $146M, with closing expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Educational only — not financial advice. Weyerhaeuser is a real estate investment trust (REIT). Net earnings of $61M are the consolidated figure attributable to common shareholders. Income taxes were a $36M benefit — the REIT structure leaves most timber income untaxed at the company level — so the printed statement steps from $25M of pre-tax income up to $61M of net earnings. This chart nets that $36M benefit into Other income (the tax line therefore reads $0) instead of drawing a negative tax band.
