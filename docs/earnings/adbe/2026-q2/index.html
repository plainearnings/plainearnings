Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

3x

AI-first products are a real business now

Recurring revenue from Adobe's AI-first products tripled year over year and passed $500M, and total ARR ended the quarter at $27.10B — including about $480M from the newly acquired Semrush.

97%

The subscription machine

Subscriptions were $6,416M of the $6,618M total, and remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue) reached $22.27B. Very little of Adobe's revenue has to be re-won each quarter.

+1%

Profit barely grew

Revenue rose 13% but net income only 1%, to $1,712M. A goodwill impairment charge and heavy spending weighed on GAAP profit; non-GAAP net income, which sets those aside, was $2.40B. Adobe still raised its full-year revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP results include a non-cash goodwill impairment charge of $0.17 per share related to the Publishing & Advertising reporting unit — it is the main reason GAAP EPS ($4.25) trails the non-GAAP figure ($5.96) by more than stock pay alone would explain.
  • Adobe closed its Semrush acquisition during the period: Semrush added roughly $480M of ARR and about $40M of subscription revenue in the quarter, and Adobe's updated full-year targets include it.
  • Adobe also announced a CFO change with this print: Dan Durn departed on June 15, 2026, with Steve Day, a 20-year Adobe finance leader, serving as interim CFO.
