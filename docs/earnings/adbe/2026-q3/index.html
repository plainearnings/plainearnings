Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+150%

AI is Adobe's growth engine now

Revenue from Adobe's AI-first products grew more than 150% year over year, and the company passed 1 billion monthly active users across its creativity and productivity apps. The AI add-ons are becoming a real business, not a demo.

$27.5B

The subscription machine keeps compounding

Total ARR — the annualized value of all subscriptions — ended the quarter at $27.50B, and remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue) reached $22.16B. Very little of Adobe's revenue has to be re-won each quarter.

+3%

Profit is not keeping up with revenue

Revenue rose 13% but net income only 3.1%, to $1,827M. Spending on R&D and sales grew, and stock-based pay of $544M counts in GAAP profit even though Adobe's non-GAAP number ($2,424M) leaves it out.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adobe's non-GAAP profit of $2,424M (EPS $6.13) excludes $544M of stock-based and deferred compensation plus $58M of intangible amortization — the gap between the two profit figures is mostly paying employees in shares.
  • Adobe raised its full-year FY26 targets alongside this print: revenue of $26,576M–$26,626M and GAAP EPS of $18.12–$18.17.
