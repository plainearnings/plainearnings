Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-9%

Sales fell across the map

Revenue fell 9% to $11.3B (down 7% without currency effects). Nike Direct fell 12% as digital sales dropped 15%, wholesale fell 7%, and every geography declined — Greater China most, at -17%.

41.5%

Discounts squeezed the margin

Gross margin fell 330 basis points to 41.5%, primarily on higher discounts, higher inventory obsolescence reserves, higher product costs and changes in channel mix. Inventories ended down 2% at $7.5B.

$794M

Profit fell 32%

Net income fell 32% to $794M and EPS to $0.54 — cushioned by the unusually low 5.9% tax rate. Operating overhead fell 13% because the year-ago quarter carried $340M of restructuring charges.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The effective tax rate was just 5.9%, versus 16.5% a year ago — Nike said a one-time, non-cash deferred tax benefit from recently finalized U.S. tax regulations related to foreign-currency gains and losses flattered the quarter's bottom line.
