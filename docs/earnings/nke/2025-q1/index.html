Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-10%

Sales fell everywhere

Revenue dropped 10% to $11,589M, with declines in every geography: North America -11%, Europe, Middle East & Africa -13%, Greater China -4%, Asia Pacific & Latin America -7%, and Converse -15%. Nike Direct fell 13% as digital sales dropped 20%.

45.4%

Margins moved the other way

Gross margin rose 120 basis points to 45.4% on lower product, warehousing and logistics costs and earlier price increases — cushioning the revenue fall. Demand creation spending rose 15% to $1,226M around the summer sports calendar.

$0.70

A transition quarter

Net income fell 28% to $1,051M and EPS 26% to $0.70; the tax rate jumped to 19.6% from 12.0% because the year-ago quarter had a one-time foreign-tax-credit benefit. Days before the report, Nike announced Elliott Hill would return as CEO on October 14, and postponed its Investor Day.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The year-ago tax comparison is flattered by a one-time item: Q1 FY24's 12.0% effective tax rate reflected a benefit from the delayed effective date of U.S. foreign tax credit regulations; this quarter's rate was 19.6%.
