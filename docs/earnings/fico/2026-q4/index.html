Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+33%

Score pricing is the growth engine

Scores revenue rose 33% to $414M — driven by a higher unit price for mortgage-origination scores, which more than offset lower mortgage volumes. B2B scoring revenue rose 39%.

$11.10

Underlying EPS is much higher

Excluding the settlement accrual, the restructuring charge and the investment gain, non-GAAP EPS was $11.10 versus $8.12 on a GAAP basis — and non-GAAP net income rose 28% to $239M.

156%

Platform software is compounding

Software platform ARR grew 72% with a 156% dollar-based net retention rate, even as older non-platform software shrank 18% — the mix shift FICO has been pushing for.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $65M accrual for a proposed settlement of the antitrust class-action lawsuits over the Scores business sits inside SG&A this quarter.
  • A $27M restructuring charge for a committed workforce-reduction plan is shown as its own running-cost line.
  • A $47.9M non-operating gain on the sale of a minority equity investment is inside other income (expense).
  • FICO gave no FY27 guidance, citing its transition to bespoke mortgage-score pricing and an uncertain mortgage market.
