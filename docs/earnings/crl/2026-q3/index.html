Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$246M

A one-off tax reversal made the quarter

Reversing acquired tax reserves — after newly enacted tax legislation and completing tax requirements in a foreign jurisdiction — added $245.6M, or $5.06 per share: a $201M benefit inside the tax line and a $44.6M benefit inside DSA running costs. That is why GAAP EPS was $6.86 while the company's non-GAAP EPS, which leaves the reversal out, was $3.09.

+4.7%

Underlying sales grew; reported sales fell

Reported revenue fell 2.2% to $982.7M because of the May 2026 divestitures (the CDMO and Cell Solutions businesses and some European Discovery sites). On an organic basis revenue rose 4.7% — DSA up 5.9% and Manufacturing up 7.4%, partly offset by RMS down 0.6%.

$3.09

Core earnings still improved

Even without the tax reversal, non-GAAP EPS rose 27% to $3.09 and the non-GAAP operating margin widened to 23.0% from 19.7%, helped by the CDMO divestiture and more safety-assessment work. Consolidated net income was $334M ($333M attributable to shareholders), vs $56M a year ago, and the company raised its 2026 guidance.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The quarter includes a $245.6M benefit ($5.06 per diluted share) from reversing acquired tax reserves, following newly enacted tax legislation clarifying the company's obligations and completion of certain tax requirements in a foreign jurisdiction: $201M was recognised as a benefit in the tax line — turning it into a $163.8M benefit for the quarter — and $44.6M as a benefit in selling, general and administrative costs for non-income tax reserves. The company treats it as one-off and excludes it from non-GAAP results.
