A supermarket keeps very little of each sales dollar: about $1.86 of every $100 became profit this quarter. Merchandise — the food and goods on the shelves — took $77.30.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+0.2%

Underlying sales barely grew

Identical sales without fuel rose just 0.2%, including a roughly 1.4-point headwind from the Inflation Reduction Act's effect on pharmacy sales. Kroger cut its full-year identical-sales guidance to 0.2%–0.8%.

+20%

Digital is the growth engine

Adjusted eCommerce sales grew 20% and Kroger Precision Marketing profit grew 24%, with eCommerce profitability improving — a bright spot that helped offset higher shrink and transportation costs in stores.

$1.09

Adjusted EPS rose 5%

GAAP EPS was $1.05. Excluding adjustment items, EPS was $1.09, up 5%, and adjusted FIFO operating profit was $1,076M versus $971M on a GAAP basis — the gap is mainly the $39M LIFO charge plus transformation and litigation items.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adjustment items excluded from adjusted EPS netted to +$26M after tax: $43M of transformation costs and $9M of merger-related litigation charges, partly offset by a $26M after-tax adjustment removing investment gains. Adjusted net earnings were $667M ($1.09 per share) versus $641M ($1.05) GAAP.
  • Merchandise costs include a $39M LIFO charge — an accounting cost that rises when the cost of goods inflates — down from last year, which helped the gross margin rate.
