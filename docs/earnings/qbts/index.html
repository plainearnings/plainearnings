Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$3.1M

Revenue went nowhere — orders did

Revenue was flat at $3.1M vs a year ago, and system sales fell 75% because no large computer sale landed. But bookings — orders that become revenue later — hit $35.5M in the first half, up 1,120%, including a $20M system sale, and unpaid contracted work (remaining performance obligations) jumped 668% to $40.7M.

$55M

Costs nearly doubled after a deal

Operating costs rose 93% to $55M. Research and development alone was $28M, up 122%, after January's acquisition of Quantum Circuits added a second, gate-model quantum team on top of D-Wave's annealing business, plus higher pay and stock compensation across the company.

-$48M

A smaller loss, for a quieter reason

The net loss narrowed from $167M to $48M — not because the business improved, but because a year ago the loss included a $142M non-cash charge from revaluing warrants ( IOUs whose value swings with the share price). Those warrants were redeemed in November 2025. The company still held $546M in cash and investments to fund the gap.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • First-half operating costs include $9.3M of non-recurring costs tied to acquiring Quantum Circuits in January 2026 (the six-month figure; the company does not split it by quarter).
  • The first half also booked a $28.4M one-off tax benefit arising from the Quantum Circuits acquisition — it sits in the six-month numbers, not in this quarter's $0.008M tax line.
