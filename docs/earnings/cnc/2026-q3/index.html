Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

92.7%

Medical costs beat the plan

The health benefits ratio — medical costs as a share of premiums — was 92.7%, better than expected, driven by Medicare, with Medicaid and Marketplace on track. Centene raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance by $0.40 to more than $5.20 and cut its full-year HBR outlook to about 90.75%.

25.7M

Fewer members, more premium each

Total membership fell to 25.7M from 28.0M a year ago — Marketplace dropped to 3.4M from 5.8M and Medicaid to 11.9M from 12.7M — but premium and service revenue still rose 0.5% to $45.1B on rate increases and growth in the Medicare drug plan, which added about a million members.

-$1.1B

Debt is coming down

Centene cut debt by $1.1B during the quarter, including fully redeeming its 2027 senior notes in October. Operating cash flow was negative $3.7B in the quarter, a timing effect of premium and claims payment cycles.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP results include $307M of pre-tax 'other adjustments' the company strips out of adjusted EPS — mainly $276M of severance from enterprise optimization and contract exits and $35M of related vendor costs, partly offset by an $18M net gain on real estate. With $161M of amortization of acquired intangibles also excluded, adjusted net earnings were $290M ($0.58 a share) versus a GAAP loss.
