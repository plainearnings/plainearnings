Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-3%

Demand cooled in the quarter

Revenue fell 3% to $9.4B. Homes closed fell 2% to 22,915 and new orders fell 7% to 18,619 homes, as rising mortgage rates, affordability constraints and cautious buyers pressured demand, in the company's words. Horton adjusted prices and buyer incentives community by community and expects incentives to stay elevated in fiscal 2027.

10.8%

Margins narrowed

Pre-tax profit fell 16% to $1.0B, a 10.8% pre-tax margin versus 12.4% a year ago. Homebuilding pre-tax profit fell 11% to $891M. Forestar (lots) added $79M of pre-tax profit and financial services $78M, which cushioned the fall.

$91M

A rental write-down weighed on profit

The rental business lost $25M before tax, including a $91M inventory impairment — writing down the value of nine multi-family rental projects to what they are now expected to fetch, booked inside cost of sales. Attributable net income was $742M, down 18%. Horton still bought back $1.4B of its own shares in the quarter and raised the quarterly dividend 11% to $0.50.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Cost of sales includes $91.4M of inventory impairment charges in the rental segment, writing down nine multi-family rental projects. Total inventory and land option charges in the quarter were $134.3M across all segments — charges taken when land or projects are worth less than Horton paid or expected, or when it walks away from land options.
