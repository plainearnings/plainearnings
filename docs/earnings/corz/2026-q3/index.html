Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$215M

Colocation is taking over

Colocation revenue rose nearly 60% in one quarter to $215M — 93% of all revenue — as 485 MW of capacity was billing at quarter end, representing over $700M of average annualized revenue. Total revenue was $233M, up 187% YoY, and colocation gross margin was 60%.

+$1.03B

A paper gain made the profit

Net income was $1,178M, but $1,034M of it was a non-cash gain as warrant liabilities fell in value, plus a $107M tax benefit. Strip those out and the operating business earned $72M. That is why diluted EPS ($0.47) is so far below basic EPS ($3.42).

$134M

The underlying business improved

Adjusted EBITDA more than tripled from last quarter to $134M, a 57% margin. Self-mining is shrinking fast (revenue down 79% YoY to $12M, at a -61% gross margin) as sites are repurposed for colocation — the segment mix is now almost entirely AI data centers.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Change in fair value of warrants and contingent value rights: +$1,034.4M — a non-cash paper gain (the warrants Core Scientific owes became cheaper as the share price moved). It is shown as a source in the chart, with the layout adapted and documented, because it is 4.4x revenue. Last quarter the same line was a $1,045.5M loss.
  • Income tax was a $106.5M benefit this quarter, mostly deferred — it added to the bottom line instead of reducing it.
  • Digital assets held were marked up by $19.3M (a gain inside operating results), partly offset by a $5.3M loss on disposing of equipment and a $1.5M write-down of assets held for sale.
