Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+5%

Growth came from the smaller machines

Total net sales and revenues rose 5% to $12,608M. Small Agriculture & Turf grew 12% to $3,383M and Construction & Forestry grew 18% to $3,618M, more than covering a 6% fall in Production & Precision Agriculture — the biggest farm machines — to $3,998M as farmers held back in a soft large-ag market.

$1.4B

Profit rose even at the cycle's low point

Net income was $1,377M in total, $1,379M of it attributable to Deere shareholders (outside partners in some subsidiaries lost $2M), or $5.10 per share vs $4.75 a year ago. Deere calls 2026 the bottom of the farm-equipment cycle and raised full-year net income guidance to $4.75B–$5.00B.

$110M

A tariff recovery flattered production costs

The quarter includes $110M of tariff recoveries ($382M so far this year) booked mainly against production costs. Leaving that one-off benefit aside, margins were helped by higher prices and volumes in two of the three equipment businesses, partly offset by higher production costs.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Deere recorded $110M of tariff recoveries in the quarter ($382M in the first nine months of fiscal 2026), mainly credited against production costs. It is a refund-style benefit, not normal selling profit, so this quarter's margins look a little better than the underlying run rate.
