For a hotel REIT, the cash story sits in funds from operations (FFO), which adds back the $187M depreciation charge: NAREIT FFO was $261M ($0.38 per share) and Adjusted FFO was $268M ($0.39 per share) — both up about 11-12% on a per-share basis. Net income attributable to Host Inc. was $70M after $1M belonging to non-controlling interests. Direct costs here combine rooms, food-and-beverage and other departmental expenses ($853M) with the $6M cost of condos sold.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.8%

Rates carried the hotels

Comparable RevPAR rose 8.8% to $219.50 — room rates up 5.7% and occupancy up 2.1 points — and Total RevPAR rose 7.3% as food and beverage spending grew.

$331M

Hotel-level profit rose 11%

Comparable hotel EBITDA reached $331M with a 25.2% margin, up 0.9 points, as higher room rates outran wage increases.

-56%

The profit drop is a missing gain

Net income fell from $163M to $71M almost entirely because last year included $122M of other gains, mostly asset sales; this year that line was a $6M loss. Operating profit actually rose 20% to $121M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other gains swung from +$122M a year ago to -$6M — asset-sale gains that did not repeat are the whole story of the net income decline.
  • After quarter-end Host bought two hotels — 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge ($202M) and Renaissance Dallas at Plano Legacy West ($172M) — and raised full-year RevPAR growth guidance to about 6.75%.
