Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+21%

Record revenue on flat capacity

Revenue rose 21% to a September-quarter record $20,186M. Passenger revenue grew 15% to $15,534M with no growth in seats — fuller planes and higher fares did the work — while cargo rose 29% and loyalty revenue grew 18%. Delta guided to roughly 20% revenue growth again in the December quarter.

+69%

Fuel ate the growth

Aircraft fuel and related taxes cost $4,351M, up 69% from $2,570M a year ago in one of the most elevated fuel environments in years. Operating income fell to $1,454M from $1,684M despite the record top line, and net income fell to $756M from $1,417M. Delta still expects full-year pre-tax profit of roughly $4.5B, absorbing about $6B of extra fuel costs this year.

$1.72

Adjusted profit held up better

GAAP EPS was $1.15, but excluding a $218M mark-to-market loss on equity investments and similar items, adjusted EPS was $1.72 and adjusted pre-tax income was $1.5B — matching last year. Delta guided to full-year EPS of $5.10–$5.60 and about $2.5B of free cash flow.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Non-operating results include a $218M mark-to-market loss on equity investments — paper losses on stakes Delta holds, which it excludes from adjusted earnings. That gap is most of the difference between GAAP EPS of $1.15 and adjusted EPS of $1.72.
