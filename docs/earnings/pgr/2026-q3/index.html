Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

88.7%

Underwriting beat its own target by a lot

The combined ratio of 88.7% means claims and expenses took 89 cents of every premium dollar. Pretax underwriting profit was $2,473M, up from $2,190M — well ahead of Progressive's standing goal of a 96% combined ratio.

+6%

Still growing, just slower

Policies in force rose 6% year over year (2.4 million more policies) and net premiums written rose 4% to $22.3B. Growth moderated from last year's pace as competition increased and Progressive cut average rates.

$534M

Old claims cost less than expected

Reserves set aside for prior-year claims proved $534M more than needed, cutting 2.4 points off the combined ratio. Catastrophe losses were $388M, up from $219M a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • No Florida policyholder credits this year: the third quarter of 2025 carried $950M of credits to Florida personal-auto policyholders, which added 4.6 points to last year's underwriting expense ratio — a big reason this year's expense ratio looks so much better.
  • Net realized losses on securities were $170M, versus gains of $295M a year ago — a $465M swing that explains why net income fell 3% even though underwriting profit rose 13%.
