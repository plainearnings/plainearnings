Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

−16% / +18%

Fewer PCs, more money

Personal Systems shipped 16% fewer units but took in 18% more revenue — memory-supply improvements let HP fulfill premium orders, and buyers are choosing AI PCs and workstations over cheap machines.

$0.11

Tariff refunds helped

GAAP EPS of $0.71 includes an $0.11 benefit from tariff refunds. Even without it, non-GAAP EPS rose 11% to $0.83, and HP raised its full-year EPS and free-cash-flow outlook.

18.1%

Printing still prints money

Printing revenue slipped 2% to $3.9 billion, but at an 18.1% operating margin it earns roughly four times the margin rate of PCs (4.6%) — the quiet profit anchor of the company.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP EPS includes an $0.11-per-share favorable impact from tariff refunds; HP's non-GAAP EPS of $0.83 includes it too.
  • The year-ago quarter contained one-time tax and litigation benefits, which is why GAAP EPS looks down 11% year over year even though non-GAAP EPS rose 11%.
  • Restructuring and other charges were $48M in the quarter.
