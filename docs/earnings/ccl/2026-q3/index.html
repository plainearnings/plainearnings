Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+2.4%

Pricing power is real

Net yields — what Carnival earns per available berth-day, in constant currency — rose 2.4% to an all-time high, more than a point ahead of guidance, on flat capacity growth.

$615M

Fuel ate the upside

Fuel cost $615M vs $451M a year ago — a $131M ($0.10 per share) drag with currency effects. Adjusted EPS was flat at $1.43 despite record revenue; management still lifted the full-year outlook by over $150M vs June guidance.

$7.6B

2027 is already selling at records

Customer deposits hit a third-quarter record $7.6B, and 2027 booked occupancy and pricing are at record levels. Carnival also bought back about $1.2B of shares this year and won an S&P upgrade to investment grade.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adjusted net income was $1,963M vs $1,920M GAAP (attributable) — the gap is $23M of debt extinguishment and modification costs, $8M of restructuring expense, $2M of ship-sale and impairment losses and $10M of other items the company strips out.
