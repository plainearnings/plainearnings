Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$10.2B

A one-off tax charge drove the headline

A European court decision on Apple's Irish taxes forced a one-time, non-cash tax charge of $10.2B. Reported EPS fell to $0.97 — without the charge it would have been about $1.64, and pre-tax profit actually rose 10%.

$25.0B

Services hit another record

Services revenue reached $25.0B, an all-time high, up 12% — more than a quarter of everything Apple sells now comes from subscriptions, the App Store and other services.

+6%

iPhone still carries the quarter

iPhone sales rose 6% to $46.2B — about $49 of every $100 Apple took in — as the iPhone 16 launch landed in the final weeks of the quarter.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A one-time, non-cash income tax charge of $10.2B sits inside the $14.9B tax line — fallout from the European General Court overturning the State Aid decision on Apple's Irish tax arrangements. It does not reflect the quarter's trading: income before taxes rose 10% to $29.6B.
