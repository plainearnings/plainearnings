Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+5.5%

Sales grew in every business

Revenue rose 5.5% to $88.4B. US sales grew 5.4% to $78.3B, international sales grew 5.1% to $7.8B, and the smaller Other businesses grew 10.1% to $2.3B. Growth came from more units sold, including specialty products and GLP-1 medicines.

4.08%

Each sale kept a little more

Gross profit jumped 22% to $3.6B, and gross margin widened from 3.53% to 4.08% of revenue. The OneOncology acquisition lifted US margins, partly offset by fast-growing GLP-1 drugs, which carry thinner margins. For a distributor that keeps only about 4 cents of each sales dollar before running costs, that shift is large.

$519M

Back to profit after a one-off-heavy year-ago quarter

Net income attributable to Cencora was $519M, versus a $340M loss a year ago, when a $724M goodwill impairment on PharmaLex crushed the quarter. Leaving out one-offs, adjusted EPS was $4.66, up 21% — the clearest sign the underlying business improved.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquisition and divestiture-related deal and integration costs were $177M in the quarter, including $94M of adjustments to RCA and OneOncology equity units and a $17M increase in the expected purchase price for RCA (contingent consideration — extra payment if the business performs).
  • Restructuring and other expenses were $65M and litigation and opioid-related expenses were $12M. Cencora treats both as one-off items outside its adjusted results.
  • Gross profit included two one-off boosts: a $38M gain from antitrust litigation settlements and a $6M LIFO credit (an inventory-accounting adjustment), partly offset by $15M of currency remeasurement expense in Türkiye.
