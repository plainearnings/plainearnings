Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+13%

Onboard spending carried the quarter

Ticket revenue fell 5% to $1,946M as pricing softened, but onboard and other revenue jumped 13% to $1,001M — guests who paid less for the fare spent more once they were on the ship.

-48%

The interest bill nearly halved

Interest expense, net, dropped to $171M from $329M a year ago after refinancing. That is the main reason net income rose 17% even though operating income fell 17% to $624M — running the ships cost more, financing them cost much less.

106.5%

Ships sailed more than full

Occupancy hit 106.5% (some cabins held three or more guests) on 5% more capacity days. Management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to about $1.70 and flagged roughly $100M of extra run-rate savings phasing in through 2028.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring costs were $2.7M in the quarter ($22.4M over the first nine months) as the turnaround program reshapes the company. Norwegian excludes them from adjusted results: adjusted net income was $514M versus $491M on a GAAP basis.
