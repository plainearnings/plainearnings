Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+6%

Beer still carries the company

Beer was $2,474M of the $2,633M total — 94% of net sales — and grew 5% on higher shipments, even though depletions (what drinkers actually took off shelves) slipped 0.6% as Modelo Especial and Corona Extra softened.

$50M

An impairment ate the growth

A $49.8M asset impairment, mainly on assets held for sale for the planned Nelson's Green Brier divestiture, plus much higher SG&A, pushed operating income down 8% to $805M despite the sales growth. On the company's 'comparable' basis operating income was $897M.

$3.32

A lighter tax bill lifted EPS

The tax provision fell to $147M from $297M a year ago, so net income attributable to Constellation rose 21% to $566M and EPS rose 25% to $3.32, even though operating income fell.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $49.8M of asset impairment and related expenses, mainly impairments on assets held for sale for the planned Nelson's Green Brier divestiture and the New Zealand wine divestitures.
  • Total 'comparable adjustments' cut GAAP operating income by $91.6M in the quarter ($71.5M after tax, $0.42 per share) — including the impairment, strategic reconfiguration and restructuring costs, and commodity-derivative settlements; comparable EPS was $3.74 vs reported $3.32.
