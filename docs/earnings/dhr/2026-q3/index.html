Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.5%

Growth came mostly from a deal

Revenue rose 8.5% to $6.6B. Diagnostics jumped 17% to $2.9B, almost entirely because Danaher bought Masimo, a patient-monitoring company that had about $1.5B of sales in 2025. Stripping out deals and currency, core revenue rose 3.0%, or 5.5% excluding respiratory-testing products, whose sales are fading.

$263M

A tax one-off flattered the bottom line

Net earnings rose 36% to $1.2B, but much of the jump was tax: Danaher booked a $263M discrete tax benefit — releasing reserves set aside for uncertain tax positions after audits were settled — which turned its tax line into a $75M benefit instead of a charge, a -6.6% effective tax rate. Profit from continuing operations was $1,207M.

19.0%

Core profitability held steady

Operating profit rose 8% to $1.2B, a 19.0% operating margin — about the same as a year ago. Operating cash flow was a strong $1.9B. Just after quarter end Danaher also closed its $1.4B purchase of StatLab, a lab-consumables maker, which joins Diagnostics in the fourth quarter.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Discrete tax benefit of $263M: Danaher released reserves for uncertain tax positions after audit settlements, cutting its effective tax rate by 23.2 percentage points and turning the quarter's tax line into a $75M net benefit. This is a one-off — it is the main reason reported profit grew so much faster than operating profit.
  • Earnings from discontinued operations of $28M were also a tax benefit: releasing reserves on old tax returns Danaher filed jointly with Veralto and Fortive before those businesses were separated from Danaher. It is folded into the other-expense line so the flow ties to total net earnings.
  • Costs include a $52M impairment charge in the Biotechnology segment — writing down the value of a facility — booked in cost of sales, and an $84M pre-tax charge in Diagnostics from writing up Masimo's inventory to fair value at acquisition and then selling it (a standard deal-accounting charge that fades as that inventory is sold).
