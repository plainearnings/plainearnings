Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

120M+

The audience kept growing

Daily active users passed 120 million and hours engaged topped 29 billion in the quarter — reach is not the problem.

+8%

Bookings slowed sharply

Bookings — Robux cash sales — grew only 8%, landing at the low end of guidance: a discovery-algorithm change that favors retention cut spending per hour, especially among younger U.S. and Canadian players.

$294M

Losses, but cash-positive

Free cash flow was +$294M despite the $185M accounting loss: players pay upfront, and $282M of the quarter's costs were stock pay, not cash.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Revenue is not the cash in: bookings were $1,557M but revenue was $1,469M. Roblox defers most bookings and recognizes them over the 27-month average paying-user lifetime, so reported revenue lags the cash by design.
  • Roblox bought back about $380M of its own shares in the quarter under a new $3B repurchase program announced in May 2026 — a use of cash, not an expense in the figures above.
