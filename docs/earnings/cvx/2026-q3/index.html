Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$4.9B

Refining carried the quarter

Downstream earnings — refining and selling fuels — rose to $4,931M from $1,137M a year ago on higher margins for refined products. U.S. refineries set a crude-throughput record of 1.09M barrels a day, running at nearly 98% of capacity.

+36%

Higher prices lifted everything

Total revenue rose 36% to $67.8B as Brent crude averaged $97 a barrel, vs $69 a year ago. Upstream (production) earned $5,698M, vs $3,302M. Production itself dipped to 3,980 thousand barrels of oil-equivalent a day from 4,086, mainly because higher prices reduce Chevron's share under some contracts and because of Middle East conflict impacts.

$5.06

Strong cash, even after one-offs

EPS was $5.06; excluding a $160M legal reserve, a $110M impairment and $24M of pension settlement costs, adjusted EPS was $5.28. Cash flow from operations was $16.0B and free cash flow $12.0B, and Chevron cut total debt by another $3.5B in the quarter.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP earnings include a $160M legal reserve, a $110M asset impairment and $24M of pension settlement costs. Excluding these and a $143M unfavorable foreign-currency effect, adjusted earnings were $10,361M, or $5.28 a share.
  • Unfavorable timing effects of $1,580M cut GAAP earnings — mainly derivatives (price hedges) marked to end-of-quarter prices in a rising market before the matching physical sales are booked.
