Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$3.1B

AI demand set a record

Revenue from AI products was a record $3.1B — roughly 43% of all semiconductor revenue — driven by custom accelerators and networking gear for data centers, and management raised full-year guidance on the strength of AI and VMware.

+175%

VMware remade the mix

Infrastructure software revenue rose 175% to $5.3B — 42% of all revenue, up from 22% a year ago — because VMware was included for a full quarter, giving Broadcom a large subscription software business alongside its chips.

-39%

Profit fell even as sales soared

GAAP net income fell 39% to $2.1B despite revenue rising 43%, weighed down by $2,343M of acquisition amortization, $345M of restructuring charges and $1,047M of interest expense. On Broadcom's non-GAAP view, net income was $5,394M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP profit carries $2,343M of amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets ($1,516M inside cost of revenue and $827M inside operating expenses) — mostly bookkeeping from the VMware deal, not cash. On Broadcom's non-GAAP view, net income was $5,394M instead of $2,121M.
  • Restructuring and other charges totaled $345M ($53M inside cost of revenue and $292M inside operating expenses), and the non-GAAP reconciliation also adds back $90M of acquisition-related costs and a $22M loss on debt extinguishment — the costs of integrating VMware.
  • The tax line was a $116M benefit, not an expense — it added to profit instead of subtracting from it. Broadcom's non-GAAP tax provision for the same quarter was $823M.
