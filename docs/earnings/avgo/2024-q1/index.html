Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+153%

VMware transformed the mix

Infrastructure software revenue rose 153% to $4.6B — 38% of all revenue, up from 20% a year ago — in VMware's first full quarter, making Broadcom nearly as much a software company as a chip company.

-65%

Profit fell even as sales soared

GAAP net income fell to $1,325M from $3,774M a year ago despite revenue rising 34%, weighed down by $2,172M of acquisition amortization, $712M of restructuring charges and $1,572M of stock-based compensation. On Broadcom's non-GAAP view, net income was $5,254M.

$4.7B

Cash flow stayed strong

Free cash flow was $4,693M — 39% of revenue — from $4,815M of cash from operations less $122M of capital expenditures. Excluding restructuring in the quarter, management put free cash flow at $5.4B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP profit carries $2,172M of amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets ($1,380M inside cost of revenue and $792M inside operating expenses) — mostly bookkeeping from the VMware deal, not cash. On Broadcom's non-GAAP view, net income was $5,254M instead of $1,325M.
  • Restructuring charges totaled $712M ($92M inside cost of revenue and $620M inside operating expenses), primarily employee termination costs from integrating VMware, and the non-GAAP reconciliation also adds back $292M of acquisition-related costs.
  • Stock-based compensation expense was $1,572M, up from $391M a year ago, including a one-time impact from aligning the vesting dates of equity awards assumed in the VMware merger and accelerated vesting for employees terminated in connection with the merger.
  • Discontinued operations added $51M, net of tax — mainly VMware's end-user computing business held for sale, which Broadcom agreed in February 2024 to sell for approximately $3.8B. Profit from continuing operations was $1,274M.
