Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+200%

VMware remade the mix

Infrastructure software revenue nearly tripled to $5.8B with a full quarter of VMware included — software was 44% of Broadcom's revenue, up from 22% a year ago.

$4.5B

A paper tax charge flipped the quarter to a loss

Moving IP rights into the U.S. as part of a supply-chain realignment triggered a one-time, non-cash tax provision of about $4.5B. The business itself earned $3.8B of operating income, and on Broadcom's non-GAAP view net income was $6,120M.

$4.8B

Cash kept pouring in

Free cash flow was $4.8B — 37% of revenue — and adjusted EBITDA reached $8.2B, 63% of revenue, even as the GAAP bottom line showed a loss.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • One-time tax provision: about $4.5B, non-cash — a discrete charge from an intra-group transfer of certain IP rights to the United States as part of a supply-chain realignment. It is the whole reason the quarter shows a GAAP net loss.
  • GAAP profit carries $2,337M of amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets ($1,525M inside cost of revenue and $812M inside operating expenses) — mostly bookkeeping from the VMware deal, not cash. On Broadcom's non-GAAP view, net income was $6,120M instead of a $1,875M loss.
  • Discontinued operations lost $443M below the line — businesses being exited; the loss from continuing operations was $1,432M.
