Cost of revenue groups the direct costs of flying: aircraft fuel ($4,843M), landing fees and other rent ($1,046M), aircraft maintenance materials and outside repairs ($806M) and regional capacity purchase — paying regional airlines to fly United Express routes ($725M). Every other operating expense line sits in operating expenses, so each statement line is counted exactly once.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+17%

A record quarter for demand

Revenue rose 17% to a record $17,875M. Premium revenue rose 17%, loyalty revenue 17%, cargo 23% and contracted business revenue 26% — and United expects even faster growth, over 20%, in the December quarter.

+62%

Fuel ate most of the growth

Aircraft fuel cost $4,843M, up $1,846M from a year ago, as the average price rose to $3.90 a gallon from $2.43. Operating income rose only 4% to $1,446M despite the record top line.

$2.54

Adjusted profit strips out sale gains

GAAP EPS was $3.14, but that includes $215M of special credits, mostly gains from selling aircraft and leasing them back. Excluding those and a $13M investment loss, adjusted EPS was $2.54 and adjusted net income was $833M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating results include $215M of net special credits: $213M of net gains on sale of assets and other special charges — primarily $294M of gains on aircraft sale-leaseback transactions, partly offset by other charges — which is why GAAP EPS of $3.14 sits above adjusted EPS of $2.54.
