Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$6.3B

A write-down made the loss

The $6,326M franchise impairment is a non-cash accounting charge — no money left the company — but it turned an operating business that produced $5,943M of Adjusted EBITDA into a $3,526M operating loss and a $2,397M net loss for shareholders.

-1.5%

Underneath Cox, sales shrank

Actual revenue rose 9.1% only because Cox was added on August 20. Pro forma — Cox counted for the full quarter in both years — revenue fell 1.5% to $16,505M, as video and Internet revenue declined, partly offset by mobile service revenue up 17.3%.

+508k

Mobile keeps growing, Internet slips

On a pro forma basis Charter added 508,000 Spectrum Mobile lines in the quarter (13.5M in total) but lost 233,000 Internet customers (34.3M in total) and 77,000 video customers. Capex was $3,027M on an actual basis.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Charter booked a $6,326M non-cash impairment of its cable franchises in the quarter — by far the largest item in these results. Without it, the operating result would have been a profit of about $2.8B.
  • Other income includes a gain of about $1.4B on extinguishing debt, from the debt exchanges tied to the Cox Transactions.
  • The Cox Transactions closed on August 20, 2026, so actual results include Cox only from that date. Charter also recorded $76M of Cox transition (integration) expenses inside operating costs.
