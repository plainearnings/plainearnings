Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$1.34

The year-ago comparison does the heavy lifting

GAAP EPS rose from $0.12 to $1.34, but a year ago earnings carried a $514M tax charge for classifying Sempra Infrastructure Partners as held for sale. Adjusted EPS, which strips these items out, rose more modestly from $1.11 to $1.40.

$205M

California disallowance bites

Regulators' proposed decisions in the 2024 rate case produced a $205M pre-tax disallowance charge ($149M after tax) at the California utilities — a reminder that regulated returns depend on regulatory outcomes.

33.3 GW

Texas demand keeps setting records

Oncor, Sempra's Texas utility stake, hit an all-time peak demand of 33.3 GW, up 6% on last year's record, and now expects its next five-year capital plan to rise about 20% to keep up with load growth.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $205M pre-tax regulatory disallowance charge at Sempra California ($149M after tax) from proposed decisions in the 2024 General Rate Case.
  • Gain on the sale of Ecogas in Mexico — $97M after tax (part of $214M of pre-tax gains on sales).
  • $65M favorable impact from foreign currency and inflation on monetary positions in Mexico, and $67M of net unrealized losses on commodity derivatives.
  • A year ago, earnings included a $514M tax charge from classifying Sempra Infrastructure Partners as held for sale — the main reason GAAP profit looks so much higher this year.
