Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+16%

Spending held up everywhere

Net revenue rose 16% (12% currency-neutral). Gross dollar volume grew 7% and cross-border volume 13% on a local-currency basis — travel and everyday spending both contributed.

+22%

Services are the growth engine

Value-added services and solutions — security, data and authentication tools sold on top of the network — grew 22% to $3.5B, well ahead of the payment network itself (+12%).

46%

Nearly half of revenue kept

Net income was $3.9B — 46 cents of every revenue dollar — even after a $202M restructuring charge. Few businesses convert revenue to profit at this rate.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $202M restructuring charge (inside general and administrative costs) and a $66M loss on equity investments are excluded from Mastercard's adjusted figures; excluding them, adjusted net income was $4.1B and adjusted EPS was $4.60.
