The striking thing: Mastercard keeps $46.10 of every $100 — a net margin most businesses can only dream of — because the network's costs are mostly people and technology, not goods. But $5,631M of the $10,551M it billed in assessments went straight back to banks as rebates before revenue was even counted.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+26%

The services side is the growth engine

Value-added services net revenue hit $3,886M — fraud tools, data and consulting now bring in 44 cents of every revenue dollar, and they grew more than twice as fast as the payment network itself (+12%).

$2.8T

Cross-border is the volume story

Gross dollar volume grew 7% to $2.8 trillion, cross-border volume jumped 14% and switched transactions rose 10% — people are spending, and spending abroad.

$3.6B

The buyback machine kept running

Mastercard repurchased 6.4 million shares for $3.6B in the quarter and paid $684M in dividends — returning $4.3B to shareholders in three months.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $174M litigation provision — settlements with merchants who opted out of the U.S. merchant class litigation — is flagged by Mastercard as a special item. Excluding it and a $104M equity-investment loss, adjusted net income was $4.3B and adjusted EPS was $4.76.
  • G&A was tempered by new government grants recognized after agreements signed in late 2025, and other income includes a one-time tax-related interest benefit — both flagged in the release as favorable one-offs.
  • Mastercard bought back 6.4 million shares for $3.6B in the quarter and paid $684M in dividends.
