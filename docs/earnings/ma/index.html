Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+19%

The services side is the growth engine

Value-added services net revenue hit $4,058M — fraud tools, data and consulting now bring in 41 cents of every revenue dollar, and they grew nearly twice as fast as the payment network itself (+11%).

$6.4B

Banks keep more of every swipe

Mastercard billed $12,118M of network assessments but handed $6,369M straight back to banks and merchants as incentives — and rebates grew 18%, faster than gross fees (+14%). The toll road keeps more traffic, but the toll-keepers bargain harder every year.

$3.0T

The world keeps swiping

Gross dollar volume rose 7% to $2,952B and purchase volume 9%, with switched transactions up 9%. Cross-border volume — the most profitable kind, from travel and cross-border e-commerce — grew 11% in local currency.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $74M litigation provision — settlements with merchants who opted out of the U.S. merchant class litigation — is flagged by Mastercard as a special item. Excluding it and a $5M equity-investment loss, adjusted net income was $4,653M and adjusted EPS was $5.31.
  • Mastercard bought back 5.3 million shares for $3.0B in the quarter and paid $763M of dividends, with $4.9B of buyback capacity left.
