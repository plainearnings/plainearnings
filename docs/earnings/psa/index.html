Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

4,764

Two deals remade the map

Public Storage closed its all-stock acquisition of National Storage Affiliates in July and bought Public Storage Canada for $1.2B (including assumed debt) in September, ending the quarter with 4,764 facilities and about 339M square feet. It expects $110–130M of yearly run-rate savings within three to four years.

92.2%

The core stores steadied

Same-store revenue rose 0.5% and same-store profit (NOI) rose 0.5% as occupancy improved to 92.2% and fewer customers moved out. Management raised full-year guidance for same-store NOI and Core FFO per share.

$4.34

Core FFO inched up

Core FFO per share (non-GAAP) rose 0.7% to $4.34 even as GAAP EPS fell 36% to $1.69. The gap is mostly the $115M of deal costs and the much larger depreciation bill on the newly bought stores — both of which FFO adds back.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Transaction and integration costs of $115M for the National Storage Affiliates and Public Storage Canada deals are the main reason GAAP net income fell 27% even as revenue rose 12%.
  • Profit also includes a $27M gain on selling real estate, a $10M foreign-currency gain and a $15M income-tax benefit — items that will not necessarily repeat.
