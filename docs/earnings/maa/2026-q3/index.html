Depreciation takes $29.82 of every $100 — almost twice the $15.37 kept as GAAP profit. That is exactly why REIT investors watch FFO, which adds most depreciation back: Core FFO was $2.10 per share versus $0.72 of GAAP EPS.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$2.10

Core FFO met expectations

Core FFO (non-GAAP) was $2.10 per diluted share, down from $2.16 a year ago, as MAA built occupancy through the quarter and kept expense growth disciplined; GAAP net income was $86M.

5.3%

Renewals are carrying pricing

Renewing-resident lease pricing rose 5.3% year over year, up 80 basis points, with record retention and turnover of just 39.6%. Same-store blended lease growth was 0.4%, and October new-lease pricing turned positive year over year for the first time in 2026.

$55M

Interest is the growing cost

Interest expense rose to $55M from $46M a year ago on a higher debt balance and rate — a key reason MAA trimmed its full-year Core FFO guidance even as October pricing accelerated.
