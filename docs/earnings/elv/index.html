Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

90.6%

Medical costs ran better

The benefit expense ratio — the share of premiums paid out as medical care — improved to 90.6% from 91.3%, driven by better performance in Medicare Advantage and individual ACA plans. Benefit expense itself fell about 1% to $37.8B even though premiums were flat.

44.9M

Fewer members, higher rates

Medical membership fell to 44.9 million, down 487,000 from a year ago: Medicare Advantage membership dropped 15.5% and Medicaid 4.9%, partly offset by growth in fee-based commercial plans. Operating revenue still rose 0.7% to $50.4B because premium rates and CarelonRx product sales rose.

$6.99

Guidance raised on both measures

GAAP diluted EPS was $6.27, up 18%; adjusted EPS was $6.99. The company raised full-year 2026 guidance to at least $22.00 of diluted EPS and at least $29.00 adjusted, and expects adjusted EPS to grow at least 12% in 2027. Net investment income jumping 66% to $1,037M helped this quarter's result.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Items the company excludes from adjusted results in the quarter: $106M of intangible amortization, $48M of net losses on financial instruments, $42M of transaction and integration costs, $18M of business optimization charges and $8M of litigation and settlement expenses — $155M in total after a $67M tax benefit. Adjusted shareholders' net income was $1,520M vs $1,365M as reported.
