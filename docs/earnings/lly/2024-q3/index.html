Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+42%

Growth, adjusting for a divestiture

Reported revenue rose 20% — but the year-ago quarter included $1.42B from selling the rights to the olanzapine portfolio (Zyprexa). Excluding that one-off, revenue grew 42%, and revenue from everything except the weight-loss and diabetes shots grew 17%.

$4.4B

Two shots are 38% of revenue

Mounjaro ($3.11B, +121%) and Zepbound ($1.26B, launched November 2023) together brought in $4.4B of the $11.4B total. U.S. sales of both were trimmed a mid-single-digit percent by wholesalers running down inventories built up in Q2.

$2.8B

The Morphic charge

Lilly booked a $2,826M acquired IPR&D charge, mostly for buying Morphic Holding, plus an $82M impairment. The charge is largely not tax-deductible, which pushed the reported tax rate to 38.9% — and reported profit to $970M (EPS $1.07), versus a $57M loss a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquired in-process R&D of $2,826M — mostly the Morphic Holding acquisition, which closed in August 2024 — plus $82M of asset impairment and restructuring charges. Lilly's non-GAAP net income of $1,065M (EPS $1.18) excludes the impairment-type charges; Lilly still counts IPR&D in its non-GAAP figures.
  • The year-ago quarter's revenue included $1,420M from the sale of rights for the olanzapine portfolio (Zyprexa) — a one-off that makes the reported +20% growth look slower than the underlying +42%.
  • Guidance moved down with this release: Lilly set 2024 revenue guidance at $45.4–$46.0B and reported EPS at $12.05–$12.55, after wholesaler inventory drawdowns trimmed Mounjaro and Zepbound sales in the quarter.
