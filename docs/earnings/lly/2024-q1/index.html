Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$2.3B

Two shots are 27% of revenue

Mounjaro ($1.8B, +218%) and Zepbound ($517M, launched November 2023) together brought in $2.3B of the $8.8B total — two versions of the same drug, tirzepatide. Demand outpaced supply, and Lilly said sales growth for its incretin medicines will mainly depend on how much it can produce and ship.

$42.4–$43.6B

Guidance raised by $2B

Lilly lifted full-year 2024 revenue guidance by $2.0B to $42.4–$43.6B, and reported EPS guidance by $1.25 to $13.05–$13.55, on the strength of Mounjaro and Zepbound and greater visibility into its production expansion for the rest of the year.

80.9%

Margins widened as prices rose

Gross margin rose 4.3 points to 80.9% on higher realized prices — mainly Mounjaro in the U.S. as savings-card dynamics faded — a favorable product mix, and, to a lesser extent, improvements in the cost of production. Operating income rose 68% to $2.5B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquired in-process R&D of $111M ($0.10 of EPS), up from $105M a year ago. Lilly counts IPR&D in both its reported and its non-GAAP figures.
  • Research and development expenses included a charge of approximately $75M associated with terminating the Verzenio prostate cancer program — the Phase 3 CYCLONE-3 trial in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, stopped for futility after an interim analysis.
