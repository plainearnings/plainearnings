Mounjaro and Zepbound together supplied $60.49 of every $100 Lilly took in — two versions of the same drug, tirzepatide, now drive three-fifths of the company.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$11.7B

One drug, three-fifths of sales

Mounjaro ($7.4B, +110%) and Zepbound ($4.3B, +123%) — both are tirzepatide — together brought in $11.7B of the $19.3B total. Company-wide volume rose 46%, partly offset by prices 5% lower.

+63%

Profits grew faster than sales

Operating income rose 63% to $8.4B. An 82.5% gross margin means most of each new sales dollar survives — and Lilly reinvested some of it in research (+26%) and launch marketing (+29%).

$80–83B

2026 guided even bigger

Lilly guided 2026 revenue to $80–83B, up from $65.2B in 2025, with non-GAAP EPS of $33.50–35.00 — betting that new factory capacity and the orforglipron pill keep the growth going.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquired IPR&D of $529M ($0.52 per share): Lilly expensed research it bought from other companies. It is a recurring feature of Lilly's deal-making, but the size swings from quarter to quarter.
  • The year-ago quarter included a one-time $300M Jardiance benefit from an amended collaboration with Boehringer Ingelheim, which makes this quarter's growth in the older medicines look worse than it was.
