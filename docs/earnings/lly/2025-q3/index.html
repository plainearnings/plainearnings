Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$10.1B

Two drugs, well over half of sales

Mounjaro and Zepbound together brought in $10.1B of the $17.6B total — Mounjaro more than doubled and Zepbound nearly tripled as Lilly added manufacturing capacity.

+54%

Growth is volume, not price

Revenue rose 54% on 62% higher volume, partly offset by prices about 10% lower — Lilly is selling far more medicine at somewhat lower realized prices.

$63.0–63.5B

Guidance raised again

Lilly lifted its full-year 2025 revenue guidance to $63.0–63.5B and reported EPS guidance to $21.80–22.50, with orforglipron — its oral GLP-1 pill — heading for regulatory submissions by year-end.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquired IPR&D of $656M ($0.71 per share): Lilly expensed research it bought with the SiteOne Therapeutics acquisition. It is a recurring feature of Lilly's deal-making, but the size swings from quarter to quarter — a year ago the charge was $2.83B.
  • U.S. tax law changes enacted in the quarter added about $350M ($0.39 per share) to the tax bill, as Lilly remeasured its deferred taxes.
