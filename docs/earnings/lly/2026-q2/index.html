Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+91%

Mounjaro nearly doubled

Mounjaro revenue rose 91% to $9.9B and Zepbound 46% to $4.9B. Volume did the work: worldwide volume rose 60%, partly offset by realized prices 13% lower.

$3.5B

Acquisition charges ate a chunk

Lilly booked $2.8B of acquired research charges (Orna, Ajax) plus $703M of deal-related impairment and restructuring. Excluding items like these, non-GAAP profit was $7.5B versus the reported $7.1B.

23.3%

The tax rate jumped

The effective tax rate rose to 23.3% from 16.5%, mainly because the acquired-research charges are not tax-deductible — one reason profit grew 25% while operating income grew 31%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Acquired IPR&D of $2,776M — research bought via the Orna Therapeutics and Ajax Therapeutics acquisitions, expensed immediately. It cost $3.03 of EPS; a year ago the same line was $154M.
  • Asset impairment, restructuring and other special charges of $703M, mostly accelerated vesting of employee stock awards and integration costs from closing the Kelonia and Centessa deals.
  • A $250M sales milestone for Jardiance, paid by partner Boehringer Ingelheim, sits inside 'All other medicines' revenue — a one-off boost to that line.
