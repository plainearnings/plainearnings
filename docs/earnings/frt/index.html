Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+14%

Re-leasing spreads are the story

Federal Realty signed 117 comparable retail leases in the quarter at rents 14% higher on a cash basis (25% on a straight-line basis) than the leases they replaced — embedded growth that shows up in revenue over coming years.

$1.87

FFO rose 5.6% a share

Nareit FFO and Core FFO were both $163.0M, or $1.87 per diluted share, and management raised full-year Core FFO guidance to $7.54–$7.58 a share.

$100M

Depreciation dominates GAAP costs

Depreciation and amortization of $100M is the single biggest expense line — the main reason GAAP net income ($69M) looks so much smaller than FFO ($163M) for a property owner.

Official earnings release (SEC EDGAR)

Educational only — not financial advice. REIT layout: the direct costs are rental expenses ($69.3M) and real estate taxes ($39.8M). Federal Realty pays no corporate income tax as a REIT — it distributes its income to shareholders instead — so the tax line is $0. Reported operating income was $116.9M including the $0.5M property-sale gain counted in other income here. Net income was $69.0M, of which $64.2M was available to common shareholders after noncontrolling interests and preferred dividends. For REITs, funds from operations (FFO) — $163.0M, or $1.87 a share — is the industry's preferred profit measure because it adds back real-estate depreciation.
