The $483M kept as profit is Carvana's consolidated net income. Of that, $194M belongs to noncontrolling interests — the other owners of Carvana Group, LLC — leaving $289M attributable to Carvana Co. shareholders, which is the figure behind the $0.39 diluted EPS.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

217,385

Record cars sold, taking share

Retail units sold rose 39% to a record 217,385 — the eighth straight quarter of roughly 40% growth — while the wider used-car market was roughly flat. Retail vehicle revenue jumped 54% to $6.14B and total revenue rose 42% to a record $8.04B.

$6,997

Each car earned a little less

Gross profit rose 32% to $1.52B, but gross profit per unit fell $365 to $6,997. Carvana chose to pass value back to buyers through cheaper loan rates, wholesale profit per car fell, and higher fuel prices and rapidly rising interest rates together cost an estimated $200 per unit.

$483M

Profit nearly doubled, despite a debt charge

Consolidated net income was $483M, up from $263M a year ago — a 6.0% net margin. Of it, $194M belongs to noncontrolling interests, leaving $289M for Carvana Co. shareholders ($0.39 diluted EPS per Class A share, vs $0.21). The quarter absorbed a $53M charge for retiring debt early; a year ago it absorbed a $120M warrant charge.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Below operating income sits a $53M loss on debt extinguishment — the cost of redeeming Carvana's Senior Secured Notes due 2030 early, including writing off related issuance costs. It is a financing one-off, not a cost of selling cars.
  • Other expense, net of $11M is also mostly one-off in nature: it mainly reflects derecognising the original Root warrants in the 2026 Warrant Exchange and a $5M fall in the value of the new warrants, partly offset by a $7M gain on its beneficial interests in securitizations.
