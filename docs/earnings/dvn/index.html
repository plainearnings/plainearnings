Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+58%

The merger doubled the company

This was the first full quarter of the combined Devon–Coterra. Production averaged 1,668,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day (oil 553,000 barrels a day), up from 853,000 a year ago, and revenue rose 58% to $6.9B.

$914M

Hedges capped the oil-price upside

Devon locks in future prices with hedging contracts (insurance against price falls). With benchmark oil at $85.63, those contracts lost $914M — mostly a $729M non-cash revaluation — cutting reported EPS to $0.88 versus core EPS of $1.42.

$4.2B

Eagle Ford sale funds returns

Devon agreed to sell its Eagle Ford assets for $4.2B, retired the last $750M of its term loan (debt is $10.6B, heading for a $9B target), and plans buybacks of more than $1B in the fourth quarter alone.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Oil, gas and NGL derivatives lost $914M in the quarter — $185M of cash settlements paid out plus a $729M non-cash drop in the contracts' value as prices rose. Excluding hedging swings, deal costs and impairments, core earnings were $1,633M ($1.42 per share) vs reported $1,008M ($0.88).
  • Devon agreed on October 8 to sell its Eagle Ford assets for $4.2B in cash (about $3.5B after tax and adjustments), closing expected in early 2027 — the proceeds are earmarked for bigger buybacks and debt reduction.
