Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+20%

The strongest quarter yet

Revenue grew 20% to $12.0B as trips rose 18% to 3.1 billion and Gross Bookings 18% to $44.2B. Mobility revenue jumped 25% and Delivery 21%; Freight was flat.

$770M

Operating profit kept compounding

Income from operations rose 18% to $770M, and Adjusted EBITDA jumped 44% to $1.8B — 4.2% of Gross Bookings, up from 3.4% a year ago. Operating cash flow was $1.8B and free cash flow $1.7B.

$6.4B

A paper tax benefit stole the headline

Net income of $6.9B is not money Uber earned selling rides: releasing a $6.4B tax valuation allowance — plus $556M of investment revaluation gains — did the heavy lifting. Costs also jumped: G&A hit $1,114M on legal and regulatory reserve charges.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Income taxes were a $6,002M benefit, not a bill — dominated by a $6.4B release of the valuation allowance on Uber's deferred tax assets, a non-cash accounting move reflecting that Uber now expects to use those past losses against future profits.
  • Net income includes a $556M pre-tax benefit from net unrealized gains on the revaluation of Uber's equity investments.
  • General & administrative expense jumped to $1,114M from $603M a year ago, including roughly $462M of legal, tax and regulatory reserve charges and settlements that Uber excludes from its non-GAAP figures.
