Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$6.54

Price beat volume

Copper sales fell 23% to 749 million pounds as Grasberg ramps up, but the price Freeport got rose 40% to $6.54 a pound — so revenue still grew 8% and operating income rose 19% to $2,353M.

105K oz

Gold sales were deferred, not lost

Gold sales were only 105 thousand ounces against production of 234 thousand, because refined gold sat in inventory at the Indonesian smelter; about 60 thousand ounces shifted into Q4, when Freeport expects 300 thousand ounces of gold sales.

$2.07/lb

Costs per pound rose

Unit net cash costs — what it costs to produce a pound of copper after by-product credits — rose to $2.07 from $1.40 a year ago, on lower volumes and higher energy and other input costs.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adjusted net income of $1,275M ($0.88 per share) excludes $101M of after-tax charges, mainly $244M of pre-tax idle-facility, recovery and restoration costs from PT Freeport Indonesia's September 2025 mud rush incident (with related depreciation).
  • Revenue includes a favorable $52M adjustment to earlier provisionally priced copper sales.
