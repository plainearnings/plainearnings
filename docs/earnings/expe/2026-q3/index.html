Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+23%

Business travel is the growth engine

Revenue from selling travel to other businesses (B2B) rose 23% to $1.7B, while the consumer brands grew 6%. Partners, not just travelers, are driving Expedia now.

34.2%

More of each dollar stuck

Adjusted EBITDA — a rough measure of operating cash profit — was $1.69B, or 34.2 cents per revenue dollar, up from 32.9 cents a year ago.

$1.0B

Profit rose even after a bigger tax bill

Net income was $1,039M in total; $1,030M belonged to Expedia shareholders ($9M to minority partners). The tax provision rose to $264M from $167M a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $35M of restructuring and reorganization charges in the quarter ($104M so far in 2026).
  • Seasonal cash pattern: operating cash flow was negative $1,157M in Q3 as traveler deposits collected earlier in the year were paid out to hotels — normal for Expedia's peak season, not a loss.
