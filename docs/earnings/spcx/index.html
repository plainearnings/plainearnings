Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+830%

Super Intelligence became the biggest business

SI revenue leapt from $813M to $7,560M in a year on new cloud services deals, the Cursor acquisition (closed August 14; $608M of revenue since) and Grok adoption. It is now 52% of all revenue. Nameplate compute reached 1.7 GW, and SI capex alone was $16.4B in the quarter.

$2.4B

Starlink is the profit engine

Connectivity earned $2,367M of operating profit on $5,248M of revenue. Consumer subscribers grew to 13.6M from 7.2M a year ago, though the average consumer paid $69 a month, down from $83 — cheaper plans and new markets are diluting the price.

$4.4B

A tax benefit topped up the profit

Net income of $4,404M was bigger than the $4,014M earned before tax, because the quarter carried a $390M tax benefit instead of a tax bill. The Space segment itself was roughly breakeven (a $7M operating loss) as Starship spending continued.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The quarter includes a $390M income-tax benefit (a year ago there was a $562M tax expense), which is why net income exceeds pre-tax income.
  • SI results include about $1.2B of share-based compensation tied to the Cursor acquisition, which SpaceX paid for with equity valued at $42.5B in GAAP purchase consideration.
