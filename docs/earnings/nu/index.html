Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$1.09B

Record profit, built on cheap operations

Net income hit a record $1.09B with a 32% ROE, and the efficiency ratio improved to a best-ever 19.3% — the bank spends less than 20 cents to make a dollar of gross profit, an almost unheard-of ratio in banking. That operating leverage is the whole investment case.

7.3%

Credit quality is the thing to watch

Expected credit losses jumped 71% year over year — far faster than the 33% loan-book growth — and the 90+ day delinquency rate rose to 7.3% from 6.9% last quarter and 6.7% a year ago. Management says risk-adjusted NIM actually improved excluding non-recurring items (12.7% vs 11.9% in Q2), but the direction of delinquencies is the quarter's one clear negative.

142.6M

The story is now bigger than Latin America

Mexico added 870,000 customers in its first quarter as a licensed bank; Colombia is now the country's largest credit-card issuer by cardholders; and Nu US launched September 10 (customers in all 50 states within weeks) with Nu Global live in 37 countries. Brazil is nearly saturated — 65% of adults already bank with Nu — so future growth has to come from these new fronts.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • No discrete one-time charge is quantified in this release. Excluding non-recurring items, risk-adjusted NIM rose to 12.7% from 11.9% in Q2 (vs 12.1% reported) — implying some non-recurring drag on margins — but the release does not size the items, so no ex-charge figures can be given.
  • Some expenses shifted into Q4: management says the improved full-year efficiency guidance (19–19.5%) reflects year-to-date performance “and a sequential increase in the fourth quarter” — Q3's cost lines are flattered slightly by timing.
  • Share-based compensation ($216.1M) and share repurchases ($500.4M) are nine-month figures from the statement of changes in equity, not Q3 alone — noted here so they aren't mistaken for quarterly numbers.
