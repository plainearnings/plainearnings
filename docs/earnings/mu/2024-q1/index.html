Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-$35M

Chips sold below cost

Gross margin was -$35M: it cost $4.76B to make $4.73B of memory. Buried in that figure is a $605M benefit from selling inventory written down in earlier quarters — without it, gross margin would have been about -$640M.

+97%

Mobile snapped back first

Mobile revenue rose 97% year-over-year to $1.29B on higher bit shipments as phone makers restocked. Compute and Networking was flat at $1.74B — the AI data-center wave had not reached Micron yet.

$1.40B

Cash flow stayed positive

Despite the $1.23B accounting loss, operations generated $1.40B of cash — depreciation alone was $1.92B. After $1.73B of net factory spending, adjusted free cash flow was -$333M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $605M benefit inside cost of goods sold from selling inventory Micron had written down during the glut (it appears in the 'unallocated' line of the segment note). Without it, gross margin would have been about -$640M instead of -$35M.
