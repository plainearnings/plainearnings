Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8%

Volume, not price, drove growth

Total revenue rose 8% to $10.4B, and product sales rose 8% entirely on volume — more prescriptions and patients — rather than price rises. Cost of sales actually fell 10% as the product mix improved.

$1.5B

A paper gain flattered the bottom line

Net income jumped 20% to $3.9B, but $1,465M of it came from mark-to-market gains on equity investments Amgen owns, booked in other income. On the company's non-GAAP view, which strips those out, earnings were $3.5B.

$2.1B

Research spending keeps climbing

R&D rose 11% to $2.1B as Amgen funds late-stage trials, including its closely watched weight-loss program. SG&A was roughly flat, so most of the quarter's extra revenue dropped through to operating income, up 47%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other income includes $1,465M of net gains from equity investments — paper (non-cash) gains from marking Amgen's stakes in partner companies to market value. They can reverse in any quarter; Amgen's non-GAAP results exclude them.
