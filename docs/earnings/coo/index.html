Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$307M

A tax settlement did the heavy lifting

Net income jumped to $433M from $98M a year ago almost entirely because of a $307M discrete tax benefit: the UK tax authority (HMRC) finished examining Cooper's 2021 transfer of intellectual property to the UK, and a reserve set aside for it was released. The total tax line was a $231M benefit.

0%

Contact lenses stood still — on purpose

CooperVision revenue was flat at $717M as the company proactively cut U.S. channel inventory, a drag it says will continue into the fourth quarter. CooperSurgical grew 2% to $349M, and overall gross margin improved to 67% from 65%.

$339M

Cash is going to buybacks

Free cash flow rose 66% to $273M, and Cooper repurchased $339M of its own stock in the quarter. The board expanded the buyback authorization from $2B to $3B, with about $1.5B still available.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $307.2M discrete tax benefit from the favorable completion of HMRC's examination of the fiscal 2021 transfer of intellectual property and related assets to the United Kingdom — it reverses a reserve recorded in 2021 and is the primary driver of the quarter's $231M total tax benefit and the jump in GAAP EPS to $2.24.
  • Smaller items the company excludes from non-GAAP results include $4.3M of strategic review costs and $2.2M of litigation expense and associated legal costs in the quarter.
