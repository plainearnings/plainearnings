Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+3%

Pricing and deals edged revenue higher

Revenue rose 3% to $11,396M. Americas Materials led, up 6% to $5,985M, while Americas Building fell 3% to $1,752M on weak homebuilding and International rose 1% to $3,659M. Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $2,682M, a 23.5% margin vs 24.3% a year ago, as pricing and acquisitions only just covered cost inflation.

$178M

An impairment took a bite out of profit

Operating income fell to $1,896M from $2,081M a year ago. The biggest single reason was a $178M loss on impairments on the Philippines reporting unit, up from $40M a year ago, alongside higher running costs. Without the impairment, diluted EPS would have been $2.09 instead of $1.86.

-22%

Bottom line fell even though cash earnings held up

Consolidated net income fell 22% to $1,184M; the amount attributable to CRH shareholders was $1,245M, or $1.86 per diluted share vs $2.21 a year ago, after a higher tax charge of $494M. CRH cut its full-year 2026 net income guidance to $3.1B-$3.3B, announced a new buyback of up to $6B, and closed the $8.5B Arcosa acquisition on October 23, after the quarter ended.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Loss on impairments was $178M in Q3 (Q3 2025: $40M), related to the Philippines reporting unit within International Solutions. It is the main reason net income fell even though Adjusted EBITDA was flat, and the company reports EPS before this charge as $2.09, vs the GAAP $1.86.
  • CRH launched Velocity, a restructuring of how it operates, in Q3 and recognised $49M of non-routine transformation costs from the program's start through quarter end. The two-year program is expected to cost about $400M in total and deliver about $400M of annual run-rate benefits by the end of 2028.
