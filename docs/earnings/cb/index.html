Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

84.1%

Underwriting stayed disciplined

The P&C combined ratio of 84.1% means claims and expenses took 84 cents of every premium dollar. Excluding catastrophes, the current-accident-year ratio was 82.6%, and P&C underwriting income was $2.1B.

$1.9B

Investments set a record

Adjusted net investment income was a record $1.89B (GAAP net investment income $1.79B, +9%), as higher interest rates lift what Chubb earns on premiums held before claims are paid.

$705M

Catastrophes explain the dip

Catastrophe losses of $705M were 2.5 times last year's light $285M. That is why core operating income eased 2% in the quarter even though the nine-month figure is a record $8.6B, up 21%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Pre-tax catastrophe losses were $705M, versus an unusually light $285M a year ago — the main reason core operating income fell 2.2% despite growth elsewhere.
  • Favorable prior-period development of $511M: reserves set aside for past claims proved more than enough, so $511M flowed back into profit (vs $361M a year ago).
  • Net realized losses of $455M on the investment portfolio are included in GAAP net income but excluded from Chubb's headline core operating income of $2,980M ($7.66 per share, non-GAAP).
