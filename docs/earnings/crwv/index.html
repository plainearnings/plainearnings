CoreWeave — Q3 2026 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q3 2026 · Quarter ended 2026-09-30

The striking thing: interest alone eats $23.11 of every $100 — four times the $5.66 of operating profit the business itself makes. And this table doesn't even show the capex: the company spent another $257.85 per $100 of revenue buying property and equipment this quarter — funded by debt, customer prepayments, and new shares, not by the business's own cash.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+168%

Growth is real and enormous — revenue nearly tripled

Revenue hit $3,661M, up from $1,365M a year ago and up more than $1B sequentially, with a $132.5B backlog and guidance raised across the board. Demand for AI computing is not the constraint.

$846M

The debt is the whole story underneath

Operating income quadrupled to $207M, but $846M of quarterly interest — on $46.9B of debt, more than double the $21.4B at year-end 2025 — turns it into a $709M net loss. Every quarter's growth is bought with more borrowing.

$7.5B

The cash burn is funded, for now

Operating cash flow was a healthy $1,975M, but capex of $9,441M means $7.5B of free-cash burn in one quarter — covered by $4.2B of convertible notes, new debt facilities, and ~$9B of customer prepayments expected in the next three months. The model works as long as customers keep prepaying and lenders keep lending.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • No one-time charges were called out this quarter. The gap between the $709M GAAP net loss and the $529M adjusted net loss is the usual recurring set: $160M of stock-based compensation and $12M of amortization of acquired intangibles, plus $11M of other adjustments (mostly investment gains) and a $19M tax effect.
  • The tax swing: this quarter carried an $83M tax expense despite a pre-tax loss, versus a $127M tax benefit a year ago — a $210M year-over-year swing that accounts for about a third of why the net loss widened by $599M.
