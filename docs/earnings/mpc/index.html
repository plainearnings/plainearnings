Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$45.70

Crack spreads exploded

The refining margin — what a barrel of crude becomes worth as fuel — hit $45.70 per barrel versus $17.60 a year ago, on strong demand and tight supply. Refining & Marketing EBITDA rose to $9.9B from $1.8B.

100%

Refineries ran flat-out

Crude capacity utilization was 100% (from 95%), with 3.2 million barrels a day of throughput — the system caught the margin wave at full volume.

$7.4B

A market windfall, not a one-off

Adjusted net income ($7.5B) was barely above GAAP ($7.4B): there was no big asset-sale gain to strip out. The profit came from the market — which also means it can reverse when spreads normalize.
