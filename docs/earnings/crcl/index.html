Of every $100 Circle took in, about $48 went straight back out to distribution partners — yet thanks to the $182M ARC token presale, it still kept $26 as profit.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+627%

A token presale did the heavy lifting

Other revenue jumped to $207M because Circle recognized $182M from preselling ARC, the token for its new Arc blockchain, which launched on September 16. Without the presale, total revenue would have fallen versus a year ago.

-8%

Lower rates are squeezing the core engine

Reserve income — the interest on the cash backing USDC — fell 8% to $658M as the return rate dropped 61 basis points to 3.5%, outweighing 8% growth in average USDC in circulation to $73.3B.

52%

Circle kept more of each dollar

After paying distribution partners, Circle kept 52% of revenue (its RLDC margin), up from 39% a year ago, as distribution costs fell 8% to $412M. Operating income rose to $208M from $81M, and Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) rose 85% to $317M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $182M of Other revenue came from the ARC token presale — gross proceeds were $242M for 807.5 million tokens at $0.30 each, with the rest still deferred. It is launch-related revenue, not recurring subscription or transaction income.
  • Operating expenses include a $9M restructuring charge for a workforce reduction of about 5%, $8M of acquisition-related costs (mainly the pending Tazapay deal) and $43M of stock-based compensation — items Circle excludes from its non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA of $317M.
