Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+5.2%

Sales grew in every category

Net sales rose 5.2% to $11.3B. Same-store sales — sales at stores open at least a year — rose 3.5%, from 2.0% more customer traffic and 1.5% more spent per visit, and all four categories grew, led by seasonal at 7.4%. It was the fifth straight quarter of traffic growth.

32.6%

Each sale kept more profit

Gross profit was $3.7B, and gross margin — the share of each sales dollar left after buying the goods — rose to 32.6% from 31.3% a year ago. Tariff refunds added about 81 basis points (0.81 of a percentage point), and distribution costs and the inventory charge known as LIFO were lower, partly offset by more markdowns and higher transport costs.

$2.48

Profit rose much faster than sales

Net income rose 34% to $550M and diluted EPS rose 33% to $2.48, because operating profit jumped 29% to $769M while store running costs stayed flat as a share of sales. Management raised full-year EPS guidance to $7.80–$8.00.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Tariff refunds, after the money Dollar General reinvested from them, added about $0.25 to diluted EPS this quarter and about 66 basis points (0.66 of a percentage point) to operating margin. The company does not expect a material benefit from them in the second half of the year, so this boost should not be treated as the normal run rate.
