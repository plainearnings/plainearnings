Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+11.3%

Growth is stronger than it looks

Revenue rose 5.2% to €4.7B ($5.4B) in reported terms, but 11.3% like-for-like — currency moves, mainly a weaker dollar, hid much of it. Highways revenue rose 9.4% and construction 7.1%, and the construction order book hit a record €18.0B.

€530M

US toll roads are the profit engine

Highways produced €530M of adjusted EBITDA on just €740M of revenue. On the US express lanes, revenue per trip rose well ahead of inflation — NTE +18.9%, NTE 35W +17.3% and LBJ +11.7% — and Toronto's 407 ETR paid out strongly as traffic grew.

€400M

Profit fell on a one-off comparison

Net profit was €400M, down from €662M a year ago — not because operations weakened (adjusted EBITDA rose 13.9% to €746M), but because H1 2025 included €275M of asset-sale gains versus €28M this half. Earnings per share fell to €0.36 from €0.75.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating profit includes a €28M net gain on disposals and impairments — mainly selling the Silvertown Tunnel stake in London (a €8M gain) and Transchile in Chile (a €22M gain). A year earlier the same line was a €275M gain, which is the main reason operating profit fell from €706M to €519M even though the businesses grew.
