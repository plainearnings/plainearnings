Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$27M

A business in transition

Revenue fell 63% to $26.7M: Bitcoin mining dropped 71% to $20.8M, partly offset by the first-ever HPC lease revenue of $5.9M from Black Pearl. The future is contracted, not current: the Barber Lake lease was expanded to 20 years, taking its contracted revenue to about $9.1B.

-$72M

Operations lost money

The reported operating loss was $71.9M. Running costs included $38.6M of compensation, $26.4M of G&A and an $13.9M paper loss on a power agreement, partly offset by a $12.4M paper gain on Bitcoin held. Adjusted EBITDA — the company's own measure — was -$10M.

+$127M

A paper gain made the profit

Net income was $31.6M only because of a $127.2M non-cash gain as Cipher's warrant liability fell in value. That is also why diluted EPS is -$0.22 while basic EPS is +$0.08: the diluted calculation strips the warrant gain back out.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Change in fair value of warrant liability: +$127.2M — a non-cash paper gain (the warrants Cipher owes became cheaper to settle as the share price moved). It is shown as a source in the chart, with the layout adapted and documented, because it is 4.8x revenue.
  • Bitcoin on the balance sheet was marked up by $12.4M (unrealized) in the quarter, and a power purchase agreement was marked down by $13.9M — both non-cash, both inside operating results as Cipher reports them.
  • Interest expense jumped to $59.0M from $1.3M a year ago as Cipher borrowed heavily to build data centers; interest income was $32.7M on its cash pile.
