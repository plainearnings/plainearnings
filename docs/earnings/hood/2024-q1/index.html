Crypto alone supplied $20.39 of every $100 of revenue — $126M of the $618M total — as digital-asset trading more than tripled from a year ago.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+232%

Crypto revenue more than tripled

Transaction-based revenue rose 59% to $329M — crypto revenue jumped 232% to $126M, options rose 16% to $154M and equities rose 44% to $39M, as customers traded actively across asset classes.

$157M

Swung to a record profit

Net income was $157M versus a $511M loss a year ago — a record quarter for profit. Revenue rose 40% while reported operating expenses fell, though that fall mostly reflects the one-time $485M pay charge in the year-ago quarter; the tax bill was just $5M because Robinhood still carried a full valuation allowance against its U.S. deferred tax assets.

$11.2B

Record net deposits

Net deposits hit a record $11.2B in the quarter — a 44% annualized growth rate against assets under custody, which rose 65% to $129.6B. Gold subscribers rose 42% to a record 1.7M and funded customers reached 23.9M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The year-ago comparison is distorted by a one-time $485M share-based compensation charge in Q1 2023, when Robinhood's co-founders cancelled their 2021 market-based stock awards. It sat in that quarter's general & administrative line, which is why total operating expenses appear to fall 52% year-over-year even though underlying spending rose — adjusted operating expenses were up 13% to $398M.
  • The $5M tax provision is unusually small: Robinhood carried a full valuation allowance against its U.S. federal and state deferred tax assets in Q1 2024. It released that allowance later in 2024, booking a one-time $369M tax benefit in Q4 2024.
