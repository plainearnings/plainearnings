Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+17%

New rates are flowing

Pennsylvania revenue rose 17% after new distribution base rates took effect July 1, 2026. Kentucky grew 11% on rates in place since January, and Rhode Island 4% on rates from September — rate cases turning into revenue across all three states.

$25B

A bigger building plan

PPL lifted its 2027–2030 capital plan to $25B, targeting about 10% annual growth in its rate base — the asset value regulators let it earn on — and raised its long-term EPS growth target to 7–9% a year through 2030. Its 2027 EPS forecast is $2.06–$2.14.

35.3 GW

Data centers keep coming

Pennsylvania's data-center pipeline grew for the 11th straight quarter to about 35.3 gigawatts in advanced planning. PPL's Invitium Energy venture agreed terms for roughly 2 GW of new generation to serve a QTS data center in Salem Township.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Special items cut GAAP EPS by $0.05, to $0.49 from ongoing EPS of $0.54 (non-GAAP): $44M after tax, mostly Rhode Island acquisition integration, a hold-harmless commitment and rate-case disallowances, plus IT and customer-system integration costs.
