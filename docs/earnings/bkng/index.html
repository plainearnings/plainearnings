Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-$900M

An FTC matter masked the growth

Booking cut revenue by $900M and accrued another $100M in general and administrative costs for a possible FTC resolution involving Priceline's disclosures, fees, customer support and billing. Without it, adjusted revenue rose 9% to $9.8B and adjusted EPS rose 17% to $4.66.

+14%

Merchant is now the engine

Merchant revenue rose 14% to $7.0B as Booking collects more travelers' payments itself, while agency revenue fell 4% to $2.5B — the ongoing shift from agency to merchant at Booking.com.

345M

Travel demand stayed resilient

Room nights rose 7% to 345 million and gross bookings rose 10% to $54.5B, even though softer long-haul international travel linked to the conflict in the Middle East weighed on demand late in the quarter.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • FTC matter: a $900M reduction in revenue and a $100M accrued loss included in general and administrative expenses, after FTC staff said in July 2026 they intended to recommend a complaint against Priceline over disclosures, fees, customer support and billing. No settlement had been agreed, litigation remains possible, and the FTC could seek more than the amounts recorded.
  • Transformation costs were $23M in the quarter, down from $105M a year ago.
  • There was no impairment charge this quarter, compared with a $457M impairment a year ago.
  • Stock split: Booking completed a 25-for-1 stock split on April 2, 2026, and all share and per-share figures in the filings are retroactively adjusted — that is why GAAP EPS is $3.61, not a pre-split figure.
