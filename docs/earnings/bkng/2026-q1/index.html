Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+27%

Merchant is now the engine

Merchant revenue rose 27% to $3.7B as Booking collects more travelers' payments itself, while agency revenue fell 2% to $1.5B — the ongoing shift from agency to merchant at Booking.com.

338M

Demand held up despite the conflict

Room nights rose 6% to 338 million and gross bookings rose 15% to $53.8B, even though management estimates the Middle East conflict cut room-night and bookings growth by about 2 points. Alternative-accommodation room nights at Booking.com rose 5.5%.

$333M

A currency swing flattered profit

A $333M foreign-currency gain on remeasuring euro-denominated debt — versus a $437M loss a year ago — is the main reason GAAP net income jumped 225%, while adjusted net income rose a steadier 10% to $902M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Foreign currency: a $333M gain on the remeasurement of euro-denominated debt and related derivatives, versus a $437M loss in Q1 2025; equity securities lost $107M in the quarter.
  • Booking received an $89M benefit from settling litigation in which it was a plaintiff, recorded as a reduction of general and administrative expenses.
  • Transformation costs were $25M in the quarter.
  • Stock split: Booking completed a 25-for-1 stock split on April 2, 2026, and all share and per-share figures in the filings are retroactively adjusted. The company also repurchased $3.6B of stock in the quarter, with $18.2B of authorization remaining.
