Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+40%

Data centers are driving growth

Revenue rose 24% to $5,617M. The fastest-growing piece was high-load customers — mainly data centers concentrated in Loudoun County, Virginia — whose regulated electric purchases rose 40% to $706M from $503M. Residential sales rose 26% to $2,157M.

$862M

Virginia is the profit engine

Dominion Energy Virginia contributed $862M of operating earnings (the company's non-GAAP segment measure), up from $679M a year ago — about three-quarters of the company total. South Carolina added $230M and Contracted Energy $134M, while Corporate and Other lost $87M.

$1.28

Near the top of guidance

Operating earnings were $1.28 a share vs GAAP EPS of $1.30 — the items Dominion strips out were small this quarter. The company said it expects to land in the top half of its 2026 operating EPS guidance of $3.45–$3.69 and reaffirmed its long-term growth, credit and dividend guidance; its release also discusses the proposed merger with NextEra Energy.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The income statement includes $19M of impairment of assets and other charges in Q3, down from $130M a year ago.
  • GAAP EPS of $1.30 vs operating EPS of $1.28: the items Dominion excludes from operating earnings — mainly gains and losses on its nuclear decommissioning trust funds and mark-to-market moves on economic hedges — netted to only about $0.02 a share this quarter. Over the first nine months those adjustments were much larger, including an $820M charge on nonregulated renewable natural gas facilities, partly offset by trust-fund gains.
