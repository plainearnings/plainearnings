MGM kept nothing this quarter: after a small tax benefit, $1.33 of every $100 of profit went to joint-venture partners, leaving a $0.40 loss per $100 for MGM's own shareholders.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-4%

Las Vegas rates were the drag

Rooms revenue fell 4% as Strip room rates softened (management said October rebounded). Casino revenue was flat at $2.3B despite the softer visitation economics.

17.1%

Macau set records

MGM China posted record quarterly revenue and a record 17.1% market share in Macau, and regional U.S. resorts grew 1% on a same-store basis — the offsets to Las Vegas softness.

$56M

Partners took the profit

Consolidated net income was $39M, but $56M of profit belonged to noncontrolling partners (notably in MGM China), leaving a $17M loss attributable to MGM shareholders.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The year-ago quarter included a $256M goodwill impairment, which is why EPS improved from -$1.05 to -$0.07 despite a softer business. This quarter had no comparable charge.
