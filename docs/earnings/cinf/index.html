Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-59%

Profit fell on investments, not insurance

Net income fell to $459M from $1,122M, almost entirely because net investment gains dropped from $853M to $20M. On the company's non-GAAP operating view, which strips those gains out, income was $443M vs $449M — flat.

90.9%

Underwriting stayed profitable

The property casualty combined ratio was 90.9% — for every $100 of premiums, about $91 went to claims and expenses, leaving an underwriting profit. It rose from 88.2% a year ago because catastrophe losses were about 7 points heavier.

+5%

Premiums and investment income grew

Earned premiums rose 5% to $2,690M, with property casualty net written premiums up 2% in the quarter on price increases and more insured exposures. Pretax investment income rose 10% to $325M, and book value per share was $105.42.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Investment gains and losses swing this company's reported profit: net investment gains were just $20M this quarter vs $853M a year ago. Excluding them after tax, non-GAAP operating income was $443M — essentially flat vs $449M a year ago — even though GAAP net income fell 59%.
  • Catastrophe losses were heavier than a year ago, adding about 7 points to the property casualty combined ratio versus last year's third quarter, which the company noted was closer to a historical third-quarter average.
