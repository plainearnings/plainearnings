The $55M net profit is the whole company's, including partners' shares: $43.3M of it is attributable to noncontrolling interests (partners in Symbotic's joint ventures), leaving $11.7M — about $1.62 of every $100 of revenue — attributable to Symbotic's own shareholders.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+22%

Growth held up, profit arrived

Revenue rose 22% to $721M, almost all of it warehouse systems ($671M). Gross margin widened to 22% from 19% a year ago, and the company swung from a $26M operating loss to a $33M operating profit. It had 77 systems in deployment and guided Q4 revenue to $760–780M.

$31M

Other income did heavy lifting

On top of operating profit came $30.6M of other income, mainly paper gains from marking strategic investments to new observable prices, less a $9.6M equity-method loss and plus a $1.1M tax benefit. That is how a $33M operating profit became a $55M net profit — investment gains, not warehouse work, supplied much of the jump.

$12M

Shareholders' slice is the smaller one

Of the $55M net income, $43.3M is attributable to noncontrolling interests — partners who co-own parts of the business, chiefly through the Walmart-related joint venture — leaving $11.7M for Symbotic shareholders, or $0.09 per diluted share. Adjusted EBITDA, the measure management steers by, more than doubled to $95M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Other income includes fair-value gains on strategic investments driven by observable price changes in the quarter (the 10-Q attributes most of the $22M YoY increase in other income to these adjustments) — paper gains that can reverse.
  • Restructuring charges were zero this quarter, vs $16.4M in the same quarter a year ago.
