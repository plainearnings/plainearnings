Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+52%

Profit rebounded while sales stood still

Earnings from operations jumped 52% to $6.6B and earnings per share rose to $4.56 from $2.59, even as revenue dipped 0.4% to $112.7B. The recovery came from spending less — on medical care and on running the company — not from growth. The company also raised its full-year 2026 outlook to at least $19.50 per share, or $20.50 on an adjusted basis.

88.4%

Medical costs finally came down

The medical care ratio — the share of premiums paid out as medical care — improved to 88.4% from 89.9% a year ago, and medical costs fell about $3.0B to $77.0B. Pricing and benefit changes helped, as did $610M of favorable reserve development: care in 2026 cost less than the money set aside for it.

48.3M

Fewer members — and Optum out-earned the insurer

UnitedHealthcare served 48.3M people, down from 50.1M a year ago: Medicare Advantage membership is down 935,000 since year-end and Medicaid rolls keep shrinking as eligibility is rechecked. Optum contributed $3.7B of the $6.6B operating profit vs UnitedHealthcare's $2.8B, helped by Optum Health swinging from $41M to $735M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Below operating earnings, UnitedHealth booked a $76M loss on subsidiaries held for sale, tied to the remaining South American businesses it is exiting; the nine-month total on that line is $209M.
  • Operating profit includes gains the company strips out of its 'adjusted' figures: $224M at the operating level from portfolio sales and South American items (gains on businesses sold, partly offset by losses on the remaining South American operations) and $74M of restructuring and other items, including a $95M reduction in loss-contract reserves.
