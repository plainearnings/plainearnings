Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

92.4%

Medical costs ate almost everything

The medical care ratio — the share of premiums paid out as medical care — jumped to 92.4%, from 87.6% a year ago, as Medicare funding cuts, Inflation Reduction Act changes and accelerating cost trends outran premium increases. Even stripping out the quarter's charge, the adjusted ratio was 91.5%. Medical costs of $82.0B left only $18.5B of gross profit on $113.2B of revenue.

$2.9B

A one-time charge crushed the quarter

UnitedHealth booked a $2,878M pre-tax charge: $799M of final cyberattack costs and $2,521M of restructuring and other actions — including a $623M reserve for loss-making contracts, $746M for real estate and workforce cuts and $329M of investment valuation losses — partly offset by $442M of divestiture gains. Optum Health swung to a $2,783M operating loss; Optum Rx was the one bright spot, earning $2,885M, up from $1,739M.

>$17.75

2026 outlook: smaller, more profitable

UnitedHealth guided 2026 revenue above $439B — a planned ~2% decline as it sheds members and right-sizes Optum Health — with earnings from operations above $24B and adjusted earnings above $17.75 a share. The medical care ratio is expected to improve to 88.8%. Cash flow from operations was $19.7B in 2025, 1.5 times net income.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A fourth-quarter charge of $2,878M before tax ($1,622M after tax, $1.78 per share): $799M of final direct costs from the 2024 cyberattack, $2,521M of restructuring and other actions — including a $623M reserve for loss-making third-party contracts, $746M for real estate rationalization and workforce reductions, $573M of contract reassessments, $329M of net valuation losses on equity securities and $250M to advance-fund the United Health Foundation — partly offset by $442M of net gains on portfolio divestitures.
  • Below operating earnings, UnitedHealth booked a $126M loss on the sale of a subsidiary and subsidiaries held for sale, tied to the remaining South American businesses it is exiting as it refocuses on U.S. health care.
  • The quarter's adjusted EPS of $2.11 (versus $0.01 GAAP) also excludes $386M of intangible amortization; adjusted earnings from operations were $3,132M versus $380M GAAP.
