Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+55%

Profit rebounded while sales stood still

Earnings from operations jumped 55% to $8.0B and EPS rose to $6.04 from $3.74, even as revenue grew just 0.4% to $112.0B. The recovery came from spending less on medical care, not from growth — and the company raised its full-year 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $19.50–$20.00.

86.7%

Medical costs came down hard

The medical care ratio — the share of premiums paid out as medical care — was 86.7%, and medical costs fell about $3.2B to $75.4B. Pricing and benefit redesign helped, as did $860M of favorable reserve development: care cost less than the money set aside for it, mostly for 2026 dates of service.

48.5M

Optum again out-earned the insurer

UnitedHealthcare served 48.5 million people and earned $3.9B on $86.0B of revenue (a 4.6% margin). Optum supported more than 120 million consumers and earned $4.0B on $65.7B of revenue, with margins expanding 1.6 points year over year.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Below operating earnings, UnitedHealth booked a $61M loss on subsidiaries held for sale, tied to the remaining South American businesses it is exiting; the six-month total on that line is $133M.
  • The medical care ratio includes $860M of net favorable prior-period development — care that cost less than the reserves set aside for it — which flatters the quarter's medical-cost improvement.
