Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+80%

Data Center hit a record

Data Center revenue reached a record $2,337M, up 80% year over year on the ramp of Instinct MI300 AI accelerators and 4th Gen EPYC sales, and Client rose 85% to $1,368M on Ryzen 8000 sales — the two AI-and-PC businesses now bring in more than two-thirds of AMD's revenue.

$36M

GAAP operating profit was barely positive

Operating income was just $36M — a 1% margin — because GAAP results carry $622M of acquisition-related amortization and $371M of stock-based compensation. On AMD's non-GAAP view, which strips those out, operating income was $1,133M. Net income of $123M also leaned on a $52M tax benefit.

-48%

Gaming and Embedded dragged total growth to 2%

Gaming revenue fell 48% to $922M on lower semi-custom console and Radeon sales, and Embedded fell 46% to $846M as customers worked through inventories — together wiping out almost all of the growth from Data Center and Client, so total revenue rose only 2% to $5,473M.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Inventory loss of $65M at a third-party contract manufacturer, related to an incident at that manufacturer's facility — included in GAAP cost of sales and added back in AMD's non-GAAP results.
