Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+69%

Data Center set the pace

Data Center revenue hit a record $3.9B, up 69%, as Instinct AI GPU shipments ramped and EPYC server CPUs kept growing — the segment is now half of everything AMD sells.

+58%

PC chips had a record quarter too

Client revenue was a record $2.3B, up 58% on Ryzen demand — together, Data Center and Client growth more than covered a 59% drop in Gaming and a 13% dip in Embedded.

$419M

Taxes ate the profit growth

Operating income more than doubled to $871M, yet net income fell to $482M from $667M a year ago: a $419M tax provision — a 47% effective rate — plus the $186M restructuring charge absorbed the gains. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.09.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring charges of $186M — one-off costs of reshaping the business, shown in the 'Restructuring & other' line net of an $11M licensing gain.
  • GAAP results carry $584M of amortization of acquisition-related intangibles ($252M in cost of sales and $332M in operating expenses) — bookkeeping from past deals, not cash. On AMD's non-GAAP view, EPS was $1.09 instead of $0.29.
