Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+80%

Data centers are the growth story

Critical infrastructure services revenue surged 80%, on data-center services demand and the Pearce acquisition (Nov 2025). Building Operations overall grew 17% to $6.9B and is now 60% of CBRE's revenue.

$184M

Asset sales flattered GAAP profit

A $184M gain on selling real estate — five times last year's — helped GAAP net income rise 22% to $443M. Strip out CBRE's list of one-offs and core net income was $599M, up 25%.

$4.7B

Much of the revenue is pass-through

Of the $11.5B top line, $4,691M is pass-through costs — subcontracted work for clients that CBRE bills back at no markup. That is why the profit margin on total revenue looks thin: the real service revenue is much smaller than the headline.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $184M gain on disposition of real estate (vs $36M a year ago) is included in GAAP operating income as reported; this page shows it in Other income. It came mainly from development asset sales, including data-center land.
  • CBRE's headline core EPS of $2.05 (non-GAAP) excludes, among other items, $76M of acquisition integration costs, $56M of non-cash amortization of acquisition intangibles and $35M of transformation costs; core net income was $599M versus GAAP net income of $443M.
