Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+13%

Electric is the growth story

Electric revenue rose 13% to $1,549M. Growth and rate recovery added $0.09 a share versus a year ago and weather and usage added $0.03, partly offset by $0.04 of higher financing costs. Gas revenue rose 3% to $640M.

$70.7B

A bigger building plan

CenterPoint raised its 10-year capital plan by $4.0B to $70.7B and pulled $1.5B of spending into 2026–2030, supporting rate-base growth of more than 14% a year through 2030 — and it sees at least $10B more of possible projects through 2035 as Houston demand accelerates.

$1.90–$1.92

Guidance raised, 2027 started higher

Full-year 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance rose to $1.90–$1.92, and 2027 guidance was initiated at $2.07–$2.09 — about 9% growth at the midpoint each year. The company extended its 7%–9% annual growth outlook through 2030.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Non-GAAP EPS of $0.53 excludes items GAAP counts: a $19M cost from temporary emergency electric energy facilities removed from rate base, $19M of mark-to-market loss on indexed debt securities (partly offset by a $17M gain on equity securities) and $2M of merger and divestiture impacts — non-GAAP income was $354M versus $331M GAAP. The sale of the Ohio natural gas business closed on October 1, 2026, just after the quarter ended.
