This was a loss quarter: merger costs and non-cash depreciation were far larger than the quarter's rent profit, so the 'kept as profit' row is negative — the loss was covered by the combined company's balance sheet.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$544M

The merger bill drove the loss

Merger-related costs of $544M — $464M of transaction costs plus about $80M of merger compensation inside G&A — cost $0.92 per share. Excluding them, Core FFO per share rose 5.1% to $1.03.

$898M

Depreciation is a merger artifact

Depreciation nearly quadrupled to $898M because Equity Residential's communities were stepped up to fair value on the merger balance sheet. It is non-cash, and FFO adds it back.

+1.7%

The apartments themselves did fine

On a merged same-store basis — both legacy portfolios as if combined all along — revenue rose 2.4% and NOI 1.7% at 95.8% occupancy.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Merger-related costs of $544M ($0.92 per share): $464M of transaction costs plus roughly $80M of merger-related compensation in general & administrative expense. The company now expects total merger-related costs of about $725M.
  • Depreciation of $898M reflects the fair-value step-up of Equity Residential's assets in merger accounting — a non-cash charge that will stay elevated.
