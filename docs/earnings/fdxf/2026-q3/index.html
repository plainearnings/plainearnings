Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+8.1%

Growth in a soft freight market

Three-month revenue rose to $2,442M from $2,259M a year ago (when the business was still inside FedEx), helped by pricing and fuel surcharges even as daily shipments softened.

$105M

Separation is not free

Separation and other costs of $105M in the quarter cut GAAP operating income to $253M from $290M a year ago. On the four-month view the company headlines, adjusted operating income was $510M, up 16.4%.

$0.98

A new standalone baseline

GAAP EPS was $0.98 vs $1.95 a year ago under FedEx — the drop mostly reflects separation costs, new standalone interest expense, and losing the old parent-company interest income.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • This page covers the three months ended September 30, 2026 from FedEx Freight's own Form 10-Q. Its earnings release headlined a one-time four-month transition period (June 1–September 30, 2026, including its last month inside the old fiscal calendar): revenue $3,281M, GAAP operating income $383M, net income $230M, adjusted EPS $2.15.
  • FedEx Freight became independent on June 1, 2026 and moved its year-end to December 31; prior-year figures are carve-out results from when it was a FedEx segment, so comparisons are approximate.
