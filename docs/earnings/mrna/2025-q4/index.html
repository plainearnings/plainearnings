Moderna — Q4 2025 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Q4 2025 · Quarter ended 2025-12-31

Moderna lost $826 million in Q4 as COVID vaccine sales fell 30%, but the loss narrowed from $1.1 billion a year ago because deep cost cuts — R&D down 31% — outpaced the revenue decline, and management now promises growth again in 2026.

R&D alone eats $114 of every $100 of revenue — Moderna spent $1.54 billion running the business against $678 million of sales. The $826 million loss was covered from its $8.1 billion cash pile, not from this quarter's sales.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-30%

Revenue fell by nearly a third — but the loss shrank

Revenue of $678M vs $966M a year ago, almost all of it lower COVID vaccine volume. Yet the net loss narrowed to $826M from $1.12B: costs fell faster than sales, with R&D down 31% and cost of sales down 39%.

up to +10%

2026: growth returns, costs keep falling

Management targets up to 10% revenue growth in 2026 — roughly half U.S., half international — with R&D of about $3.0B, cost of sales of about $0.9B, and year-end cash of $5.5–6.0B. The cost-cutting that saved 2025 is now the plan, not a one-off.

3

Three approved products, and the pipeline is doing the talking

Spikevax, mNEXSPIKE and mRESVIA are now three approved products. The flu vaccine filing was accepted in the EU, Canada and Australia but got a Refusal-to-File letter from the U.S. FDA; the norovirus Phase 3 trial is fully enrolled with a readout expected in 2026.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $144M of inventory write-downs sits inside Q4 cost of sales ($291M for the full year) — unsold vaccine stock written off as demand shrinks. It is not a separate line item, but it is a big reason cost of sales stays high even as sales fall.
  • Cash of $8.1B at December 31 includes a $600M initial draw on Moderna's $1.5B credit facility — borrowed money, not operating cash. Without it, the year-end balance would have been $7.5B, down from $9.5B a year earlier.
