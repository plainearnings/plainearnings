Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$145M

The trough quarter, by design

Moderna's year is back-loaded: fall vaccination drives the second half, and about 55% of second-half revenue is expected in Q3 alone. Q2's $145M — $87M of it in the U.S. — is the smallest quarter of the year, against $867M of research and overhead.

-7%

Costs are coming down

R&D fell 7% to $651M, SG&A 6% to $216M and cost of sales 22% to $93M. Moderna improved its 2026 operating-expense outlook by about $0.2B and raised its expected year-end cash to $4.7–5.2B.

Aug 5

The pipeline giveth and taketh away

The norovirus vaccine's Phase 3 interim analysis missed its early-success criteria, so Moderna will enroll an additional cohort. Offsetting that: its flu vaccine mFLUSIVA faced an August 5 FDA decision date, and Phase 3 melanoma data for the intismeran cancer vaccine could arrive in 2026.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Cost of sales includes $41M of inventory write-downs and $23M of costs for manufacturing capacity that sat unused — the price of keeping vaccine plants ready for the fall season.
  • After the quarter ended, Moderna paid $950M in July 2026 to settle the litigation announced in Q1 — the settlement behind a roughly $0.9B non-recurring charge in first-half cost of sales (first-half cost of sales was $1,048M, vs $209M a year ago). That payment is not in Q2's $93M.
