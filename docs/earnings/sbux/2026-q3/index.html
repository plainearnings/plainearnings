Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+7.9%

The stores are back

Global comparable store sales rose 7.9% — more transactions (+4.2%) and bigger tickets (+3.5%). North America comps rose 8.1%: the turnaround is showing up where it matters, in the stores.

−34%

China changed shape, not size

International revenue fell 34% because about 8,000 China stores stopped being company-run and became licensed under the new joint venture — Starbucks now earns royalties and JV income instead of store sales. That switch is also why total revenue fell 1% despite the strong comps.

$536M

A one-off gain did the profit lifting

Reported profit rose 87% to $1.0B, but $536M of it is the one-time gain on the China divestiture. Before that gain, operating profit was $902M — up only modestly, with roughly $0.3B of restructuring charges weighing on it.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $536M one-time gain on divesting Starbucks' China retail operations into a joint venture (net proceeds were $2.5B; about 8,000 company-run stores became licensed stores). GAAP EPS of $0.91 sits above non-GAAP EPS of $0.85 mainly because of it.
  • Restructuring and impairment charges of $303M — store closures and write-downs under 'Back to Starbucks' — versus just $21M a year ago.
