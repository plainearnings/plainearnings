Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$155M

The gap is one-time charges

GAAP operating income was $111 million; adjusted operating income was $266 million. The roughly $155 million gap is mostly one-time items — the Brazil divestiture, an impairment in Indonesia, and a litigation settlement.

−9%

Retail volume is the worry

Retail volume fell 9% as shoppers and commodity pricing worked against turkey and snack nuts — though SPAM, Applegate and Hormel chili grew. Management still cut the full-year sales outlook to $12.1–12.2 billion.

+3%

Foodservice keeps delivering

Foodservice segment profit rose 3% to $144 million on its 12th straight quarter of organic sales growth — the steadiest part of the business while retail struggles.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP results include one-time items that Hormel's adjusted results strip out: an estimated loss on the Brazil divestiture, a non-cash impairment charge on a minority investment in Indonesia, and a litigation settlement.
  • Equity in earnings of affiliates swung to a $37M loss from an $11M profit a year ago, another drag on the GAAP line.
  • The quarter's 42.3% effective tax rate (vs 22.3% a year ago) is distorted by these items and is not a guide to future quarters.
