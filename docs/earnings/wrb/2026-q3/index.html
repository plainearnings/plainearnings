Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

90.8%

Underwriting stayed profitable

The combined ratio of 90.8% means claims and underwriting expenses took about 91 cents of every premium dollar. Insurance segment pre-tax underwriting income grew 10.6% to $238M.

$384M

Investment income keeps climbing

Net investment income rose 9% to $384M as the core portfolio grew 12%. Operating income — Berkley's preferred measure, which strips out investment gains and losses — rose to $457M, or $1.17 per share.

-13%

An investment swing cut GAAP profit

Net income fell to $446M from $511M a year ago almost entirely because investments swung from $79M of gains to $18M of losses — the underlying insurance business improved.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Net investment losses of $18M (versus gains of $79M a year ago) are inside GAAP net income but excluded from Berkley's operating income of $457M ($1.17 per diluted share).
  • Catastrophe losses added 2.7 points to the combined ratio; excluding them, the current accident-year combined ratio was 88.1%.
