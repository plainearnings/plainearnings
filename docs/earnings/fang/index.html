Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$87.30

Price did the heavy lifting

Diamondback sold its oil for $87.30 a barrel, up from $64.60 a year ago. With output up about 10% to 1,034 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day, revenue rose 38%.

$2.1B

Cash poured out of the ground

Free cash flow — operating cash minus drilling and building — was $2,148M. The company returned $582M to shareholders and raised the base dividend 7.3% to $1.18 a share.

-$182M

Hedges capped the upside

Price-hedge contracts lost $182M in the quarter: when oil rises above the hedged price, the hedge pays out. It is the cost of the insurance that protects Diamondback when prices fall.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $182M loss on derivative instruments (hedges) is included in other expense above; adjusted net income of $1,649M ($5.86 per share) excludes it and similar items.
  • Diamondback bought back $106M of its 2054 and 2064 bonds below face value, booking a $4M gain on debt extinguishment.
