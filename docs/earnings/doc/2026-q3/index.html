Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+78%

Senior housing is transforming the company

Senior-housing revenue jumped 78% to $268M as Janus Living closed $1.75B of acquisitions in the quarter (with about $370M more after quarter-end). It is now a third of all revenue, up from a fifth a year ago — growth bought with acquisitions rather than won organically.

$299M

Depreciation explains the tiny GAAP profit

Depreciation and amortization of $299M — more than a third of revenue — is why GAAP EPS is just $0.02 while FFO as Adjusted, which adds depreciation back, is $0.44 a share. For landlords, the FFO figure is usually the more useful guide to cash earnings.

-2%

The core medical portfolio shrank slightly

Outpatient Medical revenue fell 2% to $315M after building sales, even though same-store property profit grew 3.1% and 846,000 square feet of leases were signed. Lab revenue rose 5% to $224M with occupancy up 270 basis points since year-end.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Transaction costs of $10M in the quarter ($43M in the first nine months), mostly tied to the Janus Living senior-housing acquisitions — they sit inside cost of revenue in this presentation.
