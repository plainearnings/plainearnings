Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$29.3B

Orders nearly doubled

Orders rose 96% organically to $29.3B, driven by Power equipment, and total backlog grew $20B in one quarter to $197B. Gas Power equipment backlog plus slot reservation agreements rose from 116 GW to 122 GW, and the company now expects at least 130 GW under contract by year-end 2026.

+51%

Grid gear is the growth star

Electrification revenue jumped 51% to $3.9B (+16% without the Prolec GE acquisition) as utilities buy substations and transmission gear, while Power revenue rose 25% to $6.1B. Electrification equipment backlog reached $42.2B, up 60% in a year.

$5.4B

Cash poured in, guidance went up

Free cash flow was $5.4B, up from $732M a year ago, lifting the cash balance to $16.8B. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $46.5–$47.5B of revenue and $16–$17B of free cash flow, and the board lifted the share-buyback authorization to $20B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Separation costs of $31M (from the spin-out from GE) and restructuring charges of $6M were excluded from adjusted EBITDA of $1,943M.
  • GE Vernova paid about $350M in the quarter to transfer part of its retiree benefit obligations to an insurer, which took over legal and administrative responsibility for them.
  • The company resolved all outstanding litigation over the Vineyard Wind offshore project in Massachusetts.
