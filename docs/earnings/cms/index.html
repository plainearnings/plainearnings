Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$525M

One restructuring swallowed the quarter

NorthStar Clean Energy took $525M of impairment, disposal and exit costs, plus a $33M write-down of equity investments. Operating income fell to $47M from $481M a year ago — almost entirely because of this one program.

$505M

The regulated utility is healthy

The electric utility alone made $505M of operating income and the gas utility $55M. CMS reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.83–$3.90 and 2027 guidance of $4.08–$4.17, targeting 6–8% long-term growth.

$1.44

Adjusted profit actually rose

Stripping out the NorthStar items, adjusted EPS was $1.44 vs $0.93 a year ago. GAAP EPS of $0.39 and the adjusted figure describe the same quarter — the $1.08-a-share gap is the restructuring.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Asset impairment and restructuring at NorthStar Clean Energy: $525M of impairment, disposal and exit costs inside operating expenses, a $33M impairment of equity-method investments, and $456M pre-tax ($1.45 per share, $1.08 after tax) in CMS's reconciliation of GAAP to adjusted earnings.
  • Because much of NorthStar is owned with outside partners, $214M of the quarter's losses were attributable to noncontrolling interests — which is how CMS reports a $91M consolidated net loss yet $121M of income available to its own common stockholders.
