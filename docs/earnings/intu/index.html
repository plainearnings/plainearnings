Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$21.4B

A record year, finished quietly

This 'quiet' quarter closed Intuit's first $20B-plus year: FY26 revenue of $21,448M rose 14%, with its 'Big Bets' — AI-driven expert and money products — growing 34% to 30% of revenue.

+20%

QuickBooks and Credit Karma did the work

QuickBooks Online accounting revenue grew 20% in the quarter and Credit Karma 16% to $743M. The always-on businesses now dwarf off-season TurboTax ($153M) — Intuit is far less of a one-season company than it used to be.

$293M

Charges and tax hid a stronger quarter

A $293M restructuring charge and a tax swing cut net income 5% to $363M despite operating income rising 40% to $475M. Read the quarter on revenue, not on the bottom line.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $293M restructuring charge (versus $1M a year ago) is the quarter's big distortion — and share-based compensation added another $507M of cost that GAAP counts.
  • Tax swung the comparison: a $177M provision this year versus a $15M benefit a year ago. That swing, plus restructuring, is why net income fell even though operating income rose 40%.
  • With this release Intuit guided FY27: revenue of $23,279M–$23,512M (+9–10%) and GAAP EPS of $20.12–$20.36; it also raised the dividend 15% to $1.38 after buying back $5.5B of stock in FY26.
