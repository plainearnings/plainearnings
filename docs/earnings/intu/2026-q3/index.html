Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$5.3B

Tax season is still the main event

Consumer revenue was $5,273M, 62% of the company. TurboTax grew 7% to $4,364M — fewer federal returns, but higher prices and fast growth in assisted tax (TurboTax Live is guided to +36% for the full year) more than made up for it.

+19%

QuickBooks' online engine keeps compounding

Global Business Solutions grew 15% to $3,285M, with the online ecosystem up 19% to $2.5B and QuickBooks Online accounting revenue up 22%. Mid-market businesses are growing north of 30%.

$1.6B

Cash comes back to shareholders

Intuit bought back $1.6B of stock in the quarter and ended it with $6.8B of cash and investments against $6.2B of debt. It also raised its full-year FY26 revenue guidance alongside this print.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Share-based compensation was $485M in the quarter — counted in GAAP profit, and the main reason non-GAAP EPS ($12.80) sits well above the GAAP figure ($11.09).
  • There was no restructuring charge this quarter (versus $1M a year ago) — unusually for Intuit's recent prints, the GAAP numbers are clean of big one-off charges.
  • Segment split: Consumer revenue is stated in the release; Global Business Solutions is the remainder of total revenue.
