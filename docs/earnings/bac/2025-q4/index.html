Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+10%

The lending engine is earning more

Net interest income reached $15,750M as average loans grew 8% to $1.17 trillion and deposits topped $2 trillion, while old fixed-rate assets repriced to today's higher rates. The net interest yield widened to 2.08%, up 11 basis points from a year ago.

+18%

Profit grew faster than revenue

Net income rose 12% to $7,647M and EPS rose 18% to $0.98, helped by a smaller credit-loss provision ($1,308M, down from $1,452M) and an efficiency ratio that improved to 61%. For the full year, net income was $30.5B and EPS $3.81, up 19%.

$8.4B

Capital is strong and going back to shareholders

The bank returned $8.4B to shareholders ($6.3B of buybacks, $2.1B of dividends) while keeping its CET1 capital ratio at 11.4%. Net charge-offs were $1,287M — 0.44% of loans, better than 0.54% a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Preferred stock dividends of $328M sit between net income ($7,647M) and the $7,319M attributable to common shareholders.
  • 4Q24 comparatives in the release reflect BAC's retrospective accounting change for certain tax-related equity investments, effective 4Q25 — year-over-year comparisons use the restated figures.
