Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-25%

Japan suspension bites

Japan Businesses' adjusted operating income fell to $559M from $750M as the Prudential of Japan sales suspension cut constant-dollar sales 36% and raised expenses. Prudential also agreed to sell its Brazil, Mexico and South Africa businesses to simplify the company.

$9.0B

PGIM keeps winning money

PGIM took in $9.0B of third-party net inflows — its best quarter in two and a half years — and earned $263M, up from $244M, on higher asset-management fees. Total company assets under management reached $1.62 trillion.

$3.9B

U.S. annuity sales boom

Retail annuity sales of $3.9B were the highest since 2013, with record sales of registered index-linked annuities. U.S. Businesses earned $1,173M on an adjusted basis, about flat with a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • GAAP net income includes $368M of pre-tax net realized investment losses and related charges (including $184M of credit-related losses) and a $32M pre-tax loss from changes in the value of market risk benefits, partly offset by $295M of pre-tax earnings from divested and run-off businesses and $104M of gains from market experience updates.
  • Prudential's headline adjusted operating income was $1,321M, or $3.77 per share (non-GAAP) — it strips out the market-driven items above.
