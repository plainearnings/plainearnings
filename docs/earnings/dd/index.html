Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+4%

The kept businesses are growing

Net sales from continuing operations rose 4% to $1,868M — organic sales (growth excluding currency and deals) rose 5%. Healthcare & Water Technologies grew 4% to $865M and Diversified Industrials grew 4% to $1,003M. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to about $7.19B of sales and $7.47–$7.52 of adjusted EPS.

$171M

Continuing profit jumped 68%

Income from continuing operations was $171M, up from $102M a year ago, as higher segment earnings met lower interest expense ($40M vs $85M) and much lower separation costs. Continuing diluted EPS was $1.24 vs $0.70; adjusted EPS, which leaves out one-offs and amortization, was $2.09.

$126M

The total bottom line is smaller than the continuing one

Total net income was $126M — $123M of it attributable to DuPont shareholders — because discontinued operations (Qnity and Aramids) lost $45M after tax. That gap is the clean-up cost of the break-up, not the performance of the business DuPont kept.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Discontinued operations lost $45M after tax in the quarter. These are the businesses DuPont has exited — the Qnity electronics spin-off (completed Nov 1, 2025) and the Aramids sale (completed Apr 1, 2026, for about $1.2B cash plus a $300M note and a stake in the buyer) — so their results no longer count as continuing operations.
  • Continuing operations include $15M of acquisition, integration and separation costs and $6M of restructuring and asset-related charges before tax ($22M of significant items in total, plus a $43M one-off tax charge inside the $108M tax line). A year ago the same separation costs were $62M, so this drag is fading.
