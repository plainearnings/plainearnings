Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-15%

A tough comparison, not a collapse

Revenue fell to $4.9B from $5.8B and net income available to common shareholders fell to $724M from $832M. Last year's quarter was unusually strong: it included earnings recognized when the 2025 GRC final decision (the regulator's ruling on what SCE can charge) was implemented retroactively to Jan. 1, 2025. Core EPS was $1.92, vs $2.34 a year ago.

$8M

Wildfire claims were quiet this quarter

Wildfire-related claims, net of recoveries, were just $8M, versus $295M a year ago, plus the usual $36M Wildfire Fund expense. SCE says it has hardened — physically strengthened against fire risk — about 90% of its distribution system in high fire risk areas, and it is pushing for durable wildfire reform with a clear liability cap for shareholders.

$545M

Interest is a growing drag

Interest expense rose to $545M from $488M a year ago as the utility borrows to fund grid investment. Even so, the company reaffirmed 2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90 to $6.20 and its confidence in 5% to 7% core EPS growth from 2025 to 2030.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Non-core items cut GAAP earnings by $14M after tax in the quarter (core earnings were $738M vs $724M of GAAP net income available to common shareholders): at SCE, $18M of pre-tax net earnings from expected wildfire recoveries and reimbursements for prior-year costs (Other Wildfire Events), partly offset by $36M of Wildfire Insurance Fund amortization; at the parent, a $1M loss on the disposition of Trio.
  • The quarter's tax line was a $203M benefit, not an expense — one reason net income of $751M held up even though pre-tax income fell to $548M.
