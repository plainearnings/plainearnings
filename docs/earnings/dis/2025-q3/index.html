Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$2.5B

Experiences carried the quarter

Experiences — parks, resorts and cruises — grew operating income 13% to $2,516M on 8% higher revenue, with domestic parks up 22% on higher attendance and guest spending. Sports profit rose 29% to $1,037M, helped by the absence of last year's $314M Star India loss.

$346M

Streaming is now a real profit engine

Entertainment operating income fell 15% to $1,022M as TV networks declined and last year's Inside Out 2 set an impossible comparison — but Direct-to-Consumer operating income swung to $346M from a $19M loss, with 183 million Disney+ and Hulu subscriptions.

$3.3B

A tax benefit doubled the bottom line

Reported net income was $5,943M ($2.92 a share), but $3.3B of it was a non-cash tax benefit recognized when Hulu's U.S. tax classification changed. Excluding such items, adjusted EPS was $1.61, up 16%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $3.3B non-cash tax benefit, recognized upon the change in Hulu's U.S. income tax classification, sits in the tax line (a $2,732M net tax benefit for the quarter) and accounts for most of the jump in reported net income.
  • Restructuring and impairment charges were $185M, primarily an impairment of an equity investment.
