Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+14%

Jets carried the quarter

Textron Aviation revenue rose 14% to $1,680M, with 51 Citation jets delivered versus 42 a year ago, and its backlog held at $8.0B. Segment profit rose only 4% to $179M, held back by manufacturing inefficiencies.

-9%

Bell is the soft spot

Bell revenue fell 9% to $933M and segment profit fell 17% to $76M on lower MV-75 program volume. After quarter-end the Army won Congressional approval for extra MV-75 funding, which helped Textron raise its full-year outlook.

$1.76

Adjusted profit beat the GAAP print

Adjusted EPS was $1.76 versus $1.56 GAAP — the gap is a $36M LIFO inventory charge (an accounting method that raises cost of sales when prices rise) and $7M of intangible amortization, both before tax. Textron raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $6.55–$6.65.
