Cost of revenue breaks down as compensation and benefits $753M, purchased services and rents $546M, fuel $420M, materials and other $224M, merger-related expenses $42M and Eastern Ohio incident costs $28M. Depreciation is the only remaining operating-expense line, so revenue less total railway operating expenses equals the reported $1,150M income from railway operations.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+14%

Record revenue — much of it fuel surcharges

Revenue hit a record $3,526M, up 14%. Volume rose 3%, and higher fuel surcharges — extra charges that rise with diesel prices — contributed eight of the fourteen points of growth.

+21%

Intermodal is the growth story

Intermodal revenue jumped 21% to $919M and merchandise 11% to $2,188M, as a tighter trucking market made rail more attractive. Coal rose 12% to $419M.

67.4%

Fuel costs squeezed the operating ratio

The operating ratio — operating costs per dollar of revenue — was 67.4%, worse than a year ago, because fuel expense jumped 77% to $420M and the quarter carried $42M of merger-related and $28M of Eastern Ohio incident costs. Excluding those items the ratio was 65.4%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $42M of merger-related expenses and $28M of Eastern Ohio incident costs are inside this quarter's operating expenses. Excluding them (and year-ago items), adjusted income from railway operations was $1.2B and adjusted EPS was $3.62 instead of $3.37.
