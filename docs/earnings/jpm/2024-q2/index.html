JPMorgan Chase — Q2 2024 Earnings Explained simply: where the money came from, and where it went

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$7.9B

One Visa gain did the heavy lifting

Exchanging its Visa Class B-1 shares added a $7.9B net gain in Corporate and $2.04 to EPS. Together with the related $1.0B Foundation donation and $546M of securities losses, the quarter's significant items added $5.0B after tax — excluding them, net income was $13.1B and ROTCE was 20%, not 28%.

+52%

The fee engine was strong too

Investment banking fees rose 52% to $2.3B, asset management fees 14% to $4.3B, and Markets revenue rose 10% (Fixed Income +5%, Equity Markets +21%) — so the underlying quarter grew even without the Visa gain.

$3.1B

Credit costs are normalizing upward

The provision was $3,052M, up 5% YoY: net charge-offs of $2,231M were up 58% YoY, predominantly driven by Card Services, plus an $821M net reserve build, mostly in Consumer.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $7.9B net gain related to Visa shares — JPMorgan tendered its 37.2 million Visa Class B-1 shares into Visa's exchange offer, receiving a combination of Visa Class B-2 and Class C shares; the gain on the Class C shares, held at fair value, was recorded in other income within noninterest revenue in Corporate. The release states it increased EPS by $2.04.
  • $1.0B donation of Visa shares to pre-fund contributions to the JPMorgan Chase Foundation — recorded in other expense in Corporate (it sits inside the 'Occupancy & other expense' bucket on this page) and reduced EPS by $0.18.
  • $546M of net investment securities losses in Corporate — the third 'significant item' flagged in the release; it reduced EPS by $0.14.
  • Excluding all three significant items, net income was $13.1B, EPS was $4.40 and ROTCE was 20%, versus reported net income of $18.1B, EPS of $6.12 and ROTCE of 28% — a $5.0B after-tax, $1.72-per-share difference, per the release.
