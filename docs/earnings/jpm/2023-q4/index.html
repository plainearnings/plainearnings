Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+19%

Interest income surged

Reported net interest income rose 19% to $24.1B — 62% of all revenue — on higher rates, higher revolving card balances and the First Republic loan book, even as deposit balances fell.

$2.9B

The FDIC charge hid a record year

The $2.9B FDIC special assessment (in Corporate, inside other expense) cut EPS by $0.74. Excluding it and $743M of net investment securities losses, net income was $12.1B, or $3.97 per share; full-year 2023 net income was a record $49.6B.

$2.8B

Credit costs normalized upward

The provision rose 21% YoY to $2,762M: $2.2B of net charge-offs — mostly normalizing Card Services losses — plus a $598M net reserve build, versus a $1.4B reserve build a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $2.9B FDIC special assessment in Corporate — the release's flagged 'significant item' for 4Q23, the bank's share of refilling the Deposit Insurance Fund after the 2023 bank failures. It reduced EPS by $0.74; excluding significant items, net income was $12.1B ($3.97 per share) instead of $9.3B ($3.04). It sits inside other expense, in the 'Occupancy & other expense' bucket on this page.
  • $743M of net investment securities losses in Corporate, primarily from sales of U.S. Treasuries and mortgage-backed securities (versus $874M of net securities losses in 4Q22, largely offset then by a $914M gain on the sale of Visa B shares).
  • First Republic (acquired May 1, 2023) contributed $647M of net income in the quarter — $1.3B of net interest income and $533M of noninterest revenue, less $890M of noninterest expense and a $125M provision for credit losses.

JPMorgan headlines 'managed' revenue of $39.9B; this page uses reported (GAAP) revenue of $38.6B — the $1,369M difference is a fully taxable-equivalent gross-up of tax-exempt income ($126M in net interest income and $1,243M in noninterest revenue) that does not change net income. For a bank, credit costs (the provision for credit losses) play the role of cost of revenue, and operating profit here is income before income taxes.
