Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+11%

Interest income did the heavy lifting

Reported net interest income rose 11% to $23.1B — 55% of all revenue — on balance-sheet mix, higher rates and higher revolving card balances, even as deposit margins compressed in the consumer bank.

$725M

One charge hid $0.6B of profit

A $725M increase to the FDIC special assessment (in Corporate, inside other expense) cut EPS by $0.19. Excluding it, net income was $14.0B, or $4.63 per share, instead of $13.4B and $4.44.

$1.9B

Credit costs fell year on year

The provision dropped 17% to $1.9B: $2.0B of net charge-offs — mostly normalising Card Services losses — were partly offset by a $72M net reserve release, versus a $1.1B reserve build a year ago.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $725M increase to the estimated FDIC special assessment in Corporate — the release's flagged 'significant item' for 1Q24, reflecting the FDIC's revised estimated losses to the Deposit Insurance Fund from the 2023 bank failures. It reduced EPS by $0.19; excluding it, net income was $14.0B ($4.63 per share) instead of $13.4B ($4.44). It sits inside other expense, in the 'Occupancy & other expense' bucket on this page.
  • $366M of net investment securities losses in Corporate, predominantly from sales of mortgage-backed securities and U.S. Treasuries (versus $868M of net securities losses in 1Q23).
  • First Republic (acquired May 1, 2023) contributed $668M of net income in the quarter — $1.3B of net interest income and $315M of noninterest revenue, less $806M of expense and a $31M net benefit to the provision — per the release; it is included in all reported figures above, not stripped out.
