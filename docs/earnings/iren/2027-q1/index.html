Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$200M

AI is now the business

AI Cloud revenue was $200M — 88% of the total — vs $7M a year ago, split between cloud services ($135M) and leasing capacity ($65M, a new line). Bitcoin mining, a year ago effectively the whole company at $233M, fell 88% to $28M as IREN redirects power and data centers to AI.

80%

Huge gross margin, bigger overhead

Delivering the service cost only $46M, leaving a $182M gross profit — an 80% margin. But running the company cost $368M, including $217M of selling, general and administrative costs and $148M of depreciation on its growing data-center fleet, turning that into a $186M operating loss.

-$263M

Paper losses deepened the bottom line

Below the operating line, $73M of finance costs and $49M of paper losses (a $28M unrealized loss on financial instruments and a $21M write-down of assets held for sale) were partly offset by $51M of interest income. A year ago those paper lines were a $665M gain and IREN made a $385M profit. It ended the quarter with $3.5B of cash to fund the build-out.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $28M unrealized loss on financial instruments is a non-cash mark-to-market charge (revaluing contracts at current prices); the same line was a $665M gain a year ago and swings sign with markets.
  • The $21M decrease in fair value of assets held for sale is a write-down of assets IREN plans to sell — largely mining-related — not a cash cost.
