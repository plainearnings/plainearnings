Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+121%

A much bigger company

Reported revenue jumped 121% to $1,767M — $896M from the acquired BDS businesses and $871M organic. Organic growth alone was 9% (10% in constant currency), ahead of guidance.

$4.10

Adjusted earnings grew 21%

Adjusted EPS rose 21% to $4.10 and Waters raised every part of its full-year 2026 guidance, saying integration and synergies are ahead of schedule.

$81M

GAAP profit is the deal's shadow

GAAP net income fell to $81M from $149M a year ago despite revenue doubling, because of roughly $400M of pre-tax acquisition accounting and deal charges running through the GAAP lines.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • BDS acquisition accounting dominates the GAAP figures: cost of revenue includes an $80M non-cash inventory and fixed-asset fair-value step-up, purchased intangibles amortization was $244M ($232M BDS-related), and Waters also recorded $54M of acquisition-related costs, $20M of restructuring and other items and $7M of ERP transformation costs. Excluding these, adjusted net income was $404M ($4.10 per share) versus GAAP net income of $81M ($0.82 per share), and adjusted operating income was $532M versus GAAP $128M.
