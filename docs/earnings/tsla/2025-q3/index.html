Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

497,099

A record quarter for volume

Tesla delivered 497,099 vehicles — its best quarter ever, up 7% YoY — and deployed a record 12.5 GWh of energy storage, up 81%, as buyers pulled purchases forward before the U.S. EV tax credit expired.

-40%

Profit fell anyway

Operating income dropped 40% YoY to $1,624M: price cuts and a higher average cost per vehicle (tariffs included) squeezed gross margin to 18.0%, while operating expenses jumped 50% on AI and R&D spending.

$4.0B

Record free cash flow

Free cash flow hit a record $3,990M — $6,238M of operating cash flow less $2,248M of capex — lifting cash and investments to $41.6B.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring and other charges of $238M sit inside operating expenses (vs $55M a year ago), and stock-based compensation was $663M — a non-cash cost inside the expense lines.
  • Other income includes an $80M gain on digital assets (Tesla's bitcoin holdings are marked to market each quarter). Excluding stock pay and digital-asset moves, non-GAAP net income was $1,770M ($0.50 a share) vs GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of $1,373M ($0.39).
