Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$1.87B

A tax entry made the profit

Net income was $2,167.3M, but $1,871.5M of it was a one-off tax benefit from releasing valuation allowances on deferred tax assets — paper profit, not cash from sales. Earnings per share of $15.72 carries the same boost.

+52%

The drug business is booming

Net product revenues rose 52% to $1,291.9M. AMVUTTRA alone brought in $1,130.4M, up 65%, as its use in the heart form of ATTR amyloidosis expanded; total TTR revenues were $1,150.0M, up 59%. Management kept its full-year 2026 guidance unchanged.

-6%

Day-to-day profit actually fell

Operating income slipped 6% to $345.8M despite higher sales. The cost of goods sold jumped 83% — AMVUTTRA carries a higher royalty rate on sales — and research spending rose 14% as big Phase 3 trials ramped up.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $1,871.5M tax benefit — the reason net income hit $2,167.3M — came from releasing valuation allowances on US deferred tax assets: an accounting judgment that past tax losses and credits are now likely to be usable. It is non-cash and one-off; without it, net income would have been about $296M.
  • Collaboration revenue fell 80% only because Q3 2025 included a one-off $300M milestone payment from Roche when the first patient was dosed in the ZENITH trial of zilebesiran.
