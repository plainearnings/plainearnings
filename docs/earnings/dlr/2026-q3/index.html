Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+21%

Rent and power pass-throughs surged

Total revenue rose 21% to $1,902M. Base rent grew 17% to $1,225M, and tenants' electricity reimbursements jumped 27% to $424M — a direct read on how power-hungry AI data centers have become.

$2.10

Cash earnings tell a richer story

Core FFO — funds from operations, the REIT cash-earnings measure that adds back depreciation — was $2.10 a share, up 11% from $1.89. GAAP EPS was only $0.48 because $539M of depreciation on buildings and equipment is a real accounting cost but not a cash one.

$49M

Deal costs are still a drag

Transaction and integration costs were $49M in the quarter (down from $87M a year ago), plus $3M of severance and legal costs. Core FFO excludes these, which is part of the gap between the $2.10 Core FFO figure and $0.48 of GAAP EPS.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Transaction and integration expenses of $49M in the quarter ($103M in the first nine months) — costs of acquiring and integrating properties and businesses; Digital Realty excludes them from Core FFO.
  • An $8M net gain on sales of properties sits in other income, below operating profit.
