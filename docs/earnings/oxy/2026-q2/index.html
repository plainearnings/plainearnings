Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$96.78

Oil prices did the heavy lifting

Worldwide realized oil prices rose 38% from Q1 to $96.78 a barrel — the single biggest reason sales jumped 53% and oil and gas pretax income reached $2.8B.

$3.0B

A free-cash-flow machine this quarter

Operating cash flow was $5.1B and free cash flow before working capital $3.0B — the best since 2022 — funding a $1.9B debt paydown and an 8% dividend increase to $0.28 a share.

1,433

Production beat guidance

Output averaged 1,433 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day, above the high end of guidance, led by the Permian and the Gulf of America.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Items Occidental calls out as affecting comparability added $368M after tax in Q2 — including $178M of midstream derivative gains, $199M of gains on asset sales and a $47M early debt-extinguishment gain. Adjusted income from continuing operations was $2,443M ($2.40 per share) versus $2,811M GAAP.
  • Debt fell fast: Occidental repaid $1.9B in the quarter, cutting principal debt to $11.8B on the way to its $10B milestone.
