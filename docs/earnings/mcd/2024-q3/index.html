Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-1.5%

Comparable sales went backwards

Global comparable sales fell 1.5%: the U.S. eked out +0.3% on bigger average checks despite slightly fewer guests, but International Operated Markets fell 2.1% (France and the U.K. were weak) and Developmental Licensed Markets fell 3.5% on the continued impact of the war in the Middle East and soft demand in China.

$6.9B

Revenue still grew

Total revenue rose 3% to $6,873M — franchised rent and royalties grew 1% to $4,094M and company-run restaurant sales 4% to $2,656M — helped by new restaurants even as existing ones sold less.

$3.13

Charges trimmed EPS

Operating income slipped 1% to $3,188M after $98M of pre-tax charges — $52M of transaction and impairment costs tied to selling the South Korea business and buying the Israel business, plus $46M of 'Accelerating the Organization' restructuring. Excluding charges, EPS was $3.23.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Pre-tax charges of $52M — transaction costs and non-cash impairment charges tied to the sale of McDonald's business in South Korea and the acquisition of its business in Israel.
  • Pre-tax restructuring charges of $46M for 'Accelerating the Organization,' the company's internal modernization program. Together the charges cost $0.10 of EPS; excluding them, EPS was $3.23.
