Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

-69%

A much smaller company on paper

Revenue fell from $3,614M to $1,128M. Pay-TV was deconsolidated on June 30, 2026 and most of Broadband and Satellite Services on July 31 — EchoStar still owns and runs those businesses, but accounting rules no longer fold their sales into its results. Wireless, down just 3% to $905M, is now the core.

$3.1B

The spectrum sale is the profit

Selling its 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz licenses to AT&T produced a $3,107M gain, and deconsolidation added a further $439M gain. Together they turned $148M of operating income into a $2,782M net profit — one-off deal gains, not money from customers.

$20.3B

Cash in, debt story changed

AT&T paid $20.25B in cash at closing, with another $2.4B placed in an FCC-mandated trust for wireless creditors. Interest expense still cost $312M in the quarter, but the spectrum proceeds transform a balance sheet that a year ago supported a $12.8B quarterly loss.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $3,107M gain on the AT&T Transactions: EchoStar sold all its 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz spectrum licenses to AT&T, closing July 28, 2026, and received $20.25B in cash (a further $2.4B went into an FCC-mandated trust for wireless creditors). Spectrum licenses are the airwaves mobile networks run on — this sale is the quarter.
  • A $439M deconsolidation gain in Q3 ($10,168M over the first nine months). EchoStar still owns and manages the deconsolidated DISH/pay-TV and broadband subsidiaries, but their results no longer count in EchoStar's reported revenue or costs — which is why revenue fell 69% year over year.
