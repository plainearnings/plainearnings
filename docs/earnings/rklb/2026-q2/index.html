Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+62%

Growth came from satellites, not just rockets

Space Systems revenue nearly doubled to $189M and is now 81% of sales. Launch Services slipped 4% to $45M even though six Electron missions flew, because two were HASTE missions whose revenue had partly been recognised in earlier quarters.

$2.36B

Backlog is at a record — and Q3 is guided higher

Backlog — orders booked but not yet flown or built — hit $2.36B, up 137% year over year, with about 45% expected to turn into revenue within 12 months. Rocket Lab guided Q3 revenue to $250M–$265M, another record if it lands.

-$49M

Still losing money, but the loss narrowed

Gross profit jumped to $85M, yet $82M of research and development plus $60M of SG&A left a $58M operating loss. The $49M net loss was smaller than the $66M loss a year earlier, and a $2.13B cash balance gives the company room to keep funding Neutron, whose first Stage 1 tank is targeted for delivery to the launch pad in Q4 2026.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • $8.6M of transaction costs sit inside SG&A in Q2 (per the non-GAAP reconciliation in the release) — deal costs tied to closing the Mynaric and Motiv acquisitions and the announced Iridium deal. They are part of the reported $49M loss, not on top of it.
