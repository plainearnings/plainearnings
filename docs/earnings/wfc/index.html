Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+11%

Balance sheet growth drove the quarter

Average loans rose 11% to $1,033B and average deposits 11% to $1,483B. Net interest income grew 5% to $12.5B and is 57% of all revenue.

16.3%

Returns climbed

Return on tangible common equity reached 16.3%, up from 15.2% a year ago, and diluted EPS rose 13% to $1.87 — helped by buying back $2.5B of stock in the quarter.

$1.0B

Credit costs rose with lending

The provision for credit losses was $1,029M, up from $681M a year ago — $983M of net charge-offs plus a $46M allowance build as commercial and auto loan balances grew.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Income tax expense included $170M of discrete tax benefits from resolving prior-period tax matters, which cut the effective tax rate to 16.4% from 18.9% a year ago.

For a bank, the provision for credit losses plays the role of cost of revenue: revenue less the provision is shown as gross profit, and pre-tax income is the operating income line. Wells Fargo net income of $5,951M is after a $14M loss attributable to noncontrolling interests (consolidated net income was $5,937M); net income applicable to common stock was $5,703M after $248M of preferred stock dividends.
