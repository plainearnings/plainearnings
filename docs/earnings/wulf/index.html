Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+51%

AI rent is now the business

Revenue rose 51% to $76.2M, and $63.0M of it — 83% — came from HPC leases (long-term rentals of powered data-center space to AI customers), up from just $7.2M a year ago. Bitcoin mining revenue fell 69% to $13.2M as power is redirected to leasing. After quarter-end, TeraWulf signed a 20-year lease with Anthropic worth roughly $19B of contracted revenue.

$710M

The profit is paper, not cash

Net income was $709.5M, but $728M of it is a non-cash gain: TeraWulf's warrant liabilities fell in value, and accounting rules book that as income. Operations themselves lost $88.2M, and the company's own Adjusted EBITDA was -$12.8M. Diluted EPS was actually -$0.03, because converting the warrants would erase the gain.

$54M

Debt is getting expensive

Interest expense jumped to $53.6M in the quarter, from $9.8M a year ago, as TeraWulf borrows heavily to build data centers. It ended the quarter with 102 MW of capacity earning rent and another 336 MW under construction.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $728M non-cash gain from the change in fair value of warrant and derivative liabilities — the biggest single line of the quarter. It reverses if the warrants gain value again.
  • A $95M gain on the sale of an equity interest in an investee — a stake TeraWulf sold during the quarter.
  • Impairment of property, plant and equipment of $9.7M, plus $3.9M of losses on equipment disposals.
