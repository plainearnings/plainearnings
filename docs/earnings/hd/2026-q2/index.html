Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+1.7%

Same-store growth is barely positive

Comparable sales rose 1.7% overall and 1.3% in the U.S. Transactions fell 1.0% while the average ticket rose 2.8% to $92.50 — fewer trips, slightly bigger baskets, and a tilt toward smaller projects.

+5.7%

Acquisitions are doing the heavy lifting

Total sales grew 5.7% — far faster than comparable sales. The gap is SRS Distribution and GMS, the professional-supply distributors Home Depot bought so its growth depends less on DIY shoppers waiting out high mortgage rates.

~12.5%

Guidance assumes the housing freeze lasts

Home Depot reaffirmed its FY26 plan: sales growth of 2.5–4.5%, comparable sales flat to +2%, and a full-year operating margin around 12.4–12.6%. Management is planning for another year of muted big-project demand.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Adjusted results exclude $178M of amortization from acquired intangibles (including $125M tied to SRS) — on that basis EPS was $4.92 instead of the GAAP $4.79.
  • The reaffirmed guidance assumes IEEPA tariff refunds will partly offset unplanned fuel, energy and other product input costs this year.
