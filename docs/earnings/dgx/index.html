Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+10%

Volume did the growing

Revenue rose 10.0% to $3.1B, driven by requisition volume — the number of test orders — up 14.5% (14.4% without acquisitions), across doctors, hospitals and direct-to-consumer channels. Revenue per requisition fell 4.2% on the mix of tests and payers. Quest raised full-year revenue guidance to $12.07B–$12.11B.

15.5%

Margins widened as costs grew slowly

Operating margin — operating profit as a share of revenue — rose to 15.5% from 13.7% a year ago, as operating income jumped 24% to $479M. The cost of running labs rose 10% with volume, but selling and admin costs rose only 4%, helped by automation and AI productivity gains.

$312M

Bottom line rose 28%

Net income attributable to Quest rose 28% to $312M and diluted EPS rose 29% to $2.78. Consolidated net income was $326M, including $14M belonging to noncontrolling interests. Leaving out amortization and small one-off items, Quest's own adjusted EPS was $3.00.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Operating costs include $3M of restructuring and integration charges (workforce reductions and combining bought businesses) and $4M of other charges, mainly an increase in the estimated amount still owed for past acquisitions. Partly offsetting them were about $6M of other gains, including $4M of insurance recoveries for legal matters booked inside selling and admin costs.
