Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$453M

Tax turned profit into loss

Viatris earned $261M of operating profit, but after $133M of interest and other expense, a $453M tax provision — on only $128M of pre-tax profit — left a $325M GAAP net loss.

+14%

China carried the growth

Greater China sales rose 14% to $703M and brands like Lipitor ($452M) stayed strong, offsetting a 2% dip in Developed Markets and supply problems in Europe and Emerging Markets.

$0.71

Adjusted profit rose

Adjusted EPS was $0.71, up 6%, and adjusted EBITDA rose 4% to $1.20B. Operating cash flow was $899M in the quarter — the underlying business still generates cash.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Purchase accounting amortization of $614M — a non-cash charge from past acquisitions, mostly in cost of sales — is the biggest reason GAAP gross margin was 38.4% while adjusted gross margin was 58.0%. Adjusted net earnings were $821M ($0.71 per share) versus the GAAP net loss of $325M.
  • Tax drove the loss: the GAAP tax provision was $453M despite only $128M of pre-tax profit, because losses in some jurisdictions earn minimal tax benefit. The company also recorded $66M of restructuring costs and $66M of other special items in cost of sales in the quarter (both excluded from adjusted results).
