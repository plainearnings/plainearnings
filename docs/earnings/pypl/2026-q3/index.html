Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$487B

The payment pipes keep growing

Total payment volume rose 6% to $487.1B and transactions rose 7% to 6.8 billion, with 440 million active accounts. The pipes are busier than ever — the question is how much PayPal keeps from each flow.

+2%

Growth is thin, and uneven

Revenue grew just 2%: transaction revenues +3%, value-added services −2%. U.S. revenue rose 5% while international fell 2%. This turnaround is being run on margins and buybacks, not top-line growth.

$1.5B

Shareholders are getting the cash

PayPal returned $1.5B to shareholders via buybacks in the quarter ($6.0B over twelve months) and declared a $0.14 dividend. Management also raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to about $5.60.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Restructuring and other charges were $255M, up from $69M a year ago — the main reason net income fell 14% even as revenue grew. PayPal's non-GAAP figures exclude $178M of that restructuring plus $33M of intangible amortization, giving non-GAAP EPS of $1.45 vs the GAAP $1.25.
  • Marks on strategic investments and crypto assets were a $3M loss — immaterial this quarter, after years of swinging PayPal's GAAP results.
