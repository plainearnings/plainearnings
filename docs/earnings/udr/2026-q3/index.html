Depreciation takes $38.32 of every $100 (the $161M real-estate line plus $3M other) — which is why REIT investors watch FFO, which adds most depreciation back: FFO as Adjusted was $0.65 per share versus $0.67 of GAAP EPS, the closeness here only because the $190M sales gain flatters GAAP.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$190M

Property deals drove GAAP profit

UDR booked a $190M gain on sale of real estate owned in a quarter of heavy deal-making: it sold two communities for $183M and closed a $2.0B joint venture with Apollo, contributing 19 communities and receiving $1.34B of equity capital. That gain is why net income jumped to $231M from $43M a year ago.

+1.6%

The rent business grew steadily

Same-store revenue rose 1.8% and same-store NOI rose 1.6%, with occupancy at 96.5% and bad debt improving. Total revenue still fell 1% because sold properties no longer contribute rent.

$0.65

FFOA flat, guidance raised

FFO as Adjusted was $0.65 per diluted share, flat year over year, and FFO was $0.62. UDR raised its full-year net income guidance sharply — to $1.58–$1.60 a share — on the sales gains, and nudged FFOA guidance up to $2.54–$2.56.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • Results include a $190M gain on sale of real estate owned — UDR sold two apartment communities (Seattle and Nashville, 412 homes, $183M of gross proceeds) and closed its Apollo joint venture in the quarter. Excluding such gains is exactly what FFO does, which is why FFO per share ($0.62) barely moved while GAAP EPS jumped to $0.67 from $0.12.
