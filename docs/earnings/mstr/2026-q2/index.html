Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

−$8.3B

A paper loss, not a cash loss

Bitcoin fell during the quarter, so accounting rules wrote the stash down by $8.3B. Strategy actually bought 85,296 more Bitcoin in Q2 for $6.4B and ended June holding 846,000.

$122M

The software business is steady

Software revenue grew 7% to $122M with a 67% gross margin — subscriptions ($63M) are now the biggest piece as licenses shrink. Before the Bitcoin line, software gross profit roughly covered running costs.

−$24.45

Common holders bear it all

After $401M of dividends on Strategy's preferred stock, the loss attributable to common stockholders was $8.62B, or $24.45 per share.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $8.3B Bitcoin loss is not a sale — it is a mark-to-market revaluation under accounting rule ASU 2023-08 (in force since January 1, 2025), which runs Bitcoin's price changes through earnings every quarter. It will be back next quarter, as a gain or a loss: over the first half of 2026 this same line nets to a $22.8B loss.
  • Between net loss and what common shareholders 'got': $400.7M of dividends on Strategy's preferred stock — leaving −$8,620.3M attributable to common stockholders.
  • Strategy repurchased $1.5B of its 2029 convertible notes for about $1.38B in cash in May 2026, booking a $113.9M gain on debt extinguishment — it sits in other income above.
