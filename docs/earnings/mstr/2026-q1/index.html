Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

−$14.5B

A paper loss, not a cash loss

Bitcoin fell during the quarter, so accounting rules wrote the stash down by $14.5B. Strategy actually bought 89,599 more Bitcoin in Q1 for $7.3B and ended March holding 762,099.

$124M

The software business is steady

Software revenue grew 12% to $124M with a 67% gross margin — subscriptions ($59M) are now the biggest piece as licenses shrink. Before the Bitcoin line, the software business lost about $15M.

−$38.25

Common holders bear it all

After $230M of dividends on Strategy's preferred stock, the loss attributable to common stockholders was $12.77B, or $38.25 per share. A $1.9B tax benefit softened the headline loss.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $14.5B Bitcoin loss is not a sale — it is a mark-to-market revaluation under accounting rule ASU 2023-08 (in force since January 1, 2025), which runs Bitcoin's price changes through earnings every quarter. It will be back next quarter, as a gain or a loss.
  • The writedown generated a $1.9B income-tax benefit — mostly deferred taxes, which reverse if Bitcoin's price recovers.
  • Between net loss and what common shareholders 'got': $229.5M of dividends on Strategy's preferred stock — leaving −$12,772.2M attributable to common stockholders.
