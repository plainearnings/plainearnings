A different kind of report — read this first

Strategy (the company formerly known as MicroStrategy) breaks three of our usual rules, so we adapted the layout — openly:

  • The chart starts from total money in — $21.0B — not revenue. Strategy's official revenue is $125M, but accounting rules place a $20.9B Bitcoin gain inside its operating profit. Starting from revenue would hide 99.4% of the story.
  • Follow the orange. Bitcoin money is orange; money customers paid is blue. The orange river is one paper gain flowing untouched through the chart to the bottom line — it is never cash.
  • The “Follow $100” table is per $100 of total money in, not per $100 of revenue, and chart percentages are shares of total money in.
  • Nothing is hidden: the official figures are revenue $125.4M, gross profit $89.6M, operating income $20,909.8M and net income $19,533.0M — and all three of our balance checks pass on the adapted flow.

Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

$20.9B

A paper gain, not sales

Bitcoin's rising price added $20.9B to the value of Strategy's stash this quarter. No Bitcoin was sold and no cash changed hands. The same line was a net $1.9B loss over the first nine months of 2026 — it swings violently with Bitcoin's price, in both directions.

$125M

The software business roughly broke even

Software brought in $125.4M (−3% YoY) and earned $89.6M after direct costs — against $92.2M of running costs. Before the Bitcoin gain, operations lost $2.6M. The business is 0.6% of the money in this chart.

848,517

The bet keeps growing

Strategy held 848,517 Bitcoin as of October 25, 2026 (847,666 at quarter-end, carried on the books at $70.8B) — accumulated largely with borrowed money and new stock issuance. The company is now, in effect, a Bitcoin holding company with a small software business attached.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • The $20.9B Bitcoin gain is not a one-off sale — it is a mark-to-market revaluation under accounting rule ASU 2023-08 (in force since January 1, 2025), which runs Bitcoin's price changes through earnings every quarter. It will be back next quarter, as a gain or a loss: over the first nine months of 2026 this same line nets to a $1.9B loss, because Q3's gain only partly undid earlier drops.
  • Between net income and what common shareholders “got”: $419.6M of dividends on Strategy's preferred stock, partly offset by a $5.5M deemed contribution from preferred repurchases — leaving $19,118.9M attributable to common stockholders.
