Three things to take away

If you only remember three numbers from this quarter, make it these.

+49%

Orders are outrunning production

Orders hit $19.0B — including the largest LEAP agreement ever (1,000+ LEAP-1A engines for IndiGo) — as airlines lock in engines and long-term service deals faster than GE can build them.

+21%

Commercial engines & services boomed

CES revenue rose 21% to $10.3B with a 27.9% segment profit margin. Services — maintaining the huge installed fleet — are the profit engine, and LEAP deliveries grew 26%.

$2.32

Guidance went up again

Continuing GAAP EPS was $2.32 (+14%) and adjusted EPS $2.25 (+36%); GE raised full-year guidance on year-to-date orders, revenue and deliveries all up over 20%.

One-time items to know about

Things that make this quarter look different from a normal one — so you don't mistake them for the trend.

  • A $124M loss from discontinued operations (legacy businesses) sits below the tax line and is included in the chart's other-income line, as explained there.
  • Other income includes $117M of gains on retained and sold ownership interests and other equity securities.
  • The run-off insurance business contributed $833M of revenue and $1,043M of costs and expenses inside the GAAP totals; GE's adjusted figures exclude it.
